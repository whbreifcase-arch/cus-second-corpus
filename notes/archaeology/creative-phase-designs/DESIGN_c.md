# THE GREEN ROAD

A skirmish game for two to four people, one hour, on a coffee table, with the
miniatures you already own and a handful of coins.

---

## WHAT YOU NEED

Eight six-sided dice. A pile of small coins for wounds. A pile of larger or
brighter coins for Guard — they must look different across the room. Eight to ten
miniatures of any kind. Index cards, one per figure, and a pencil. A coin to flip.

Terrain is whatever is in the room. Books are banks. A folded tea towel is water.
A belt laid flat is a wall. A cereal box is a barn.

**The measure is a hand** — the youngest player's open hand, thumb-tip to
little-finger-tip. Everyone uses that hand. You never lay it on the table to check;
you look, and you say. If two people look and disagree, **flip the coin.** The coin
is faster than the argument and the argument is not the game.

---

## 1 · THE ROLL

There is one roll in this game.

> **Take a handful of dice. Every 4, 5 or 6 is a hit. Every hit is a wound.**

The number you need is **4**. It is always 4. Nothing in this game ever changes it,
and nothing is ever added to a die.

Everything that matters changes **how many dice you pick up**, and every one of those
things is something you can see on the table without asking anybody.

### Building the handful

Start with the figure's **Skill**, printed on its card. That is 1, 2 or 3 dice.

Then look at the table and ask five questions. Each is a yes or a no, and each is
answered by looking, not by remembering.

**Take a die for each of these:**

- **HIGH** — your figure's feet are clearly above theirs. A book, a step, a bank, the
  ox's back.
- **BLIND SIDE** — you are behind the line across their shoulders. Figures face where
  they are pointed, and they only turn on their own activation.
- **ALONE** — no friend of theirs is standing within a hand of them.

**Put a die back for each of these:**

- **COVER** — something solid interrupts the line from you to them. A wall, a cart, an ox.
- **FAR** — they are more than about four hands off. Half the table.

**If your handful comes to nothing, you cannot make that attack.** Not "roll one
anyway." You are standing in the wrong place. Move, or do something else.

There is no upper limit and there is no cap. The best shot on the road is seven
dice; Fisk can reach eight if he has arranged something remarkable. Both are very hard
to arrange, which is the point.

### Damage

**One hit, one wound. Put a coin on the base.**

When the coins on a figure equal its **Grit**, it goes **Down** — lay the figure on
its side. Wounds past that are lost. Overkill buys nothing.

Down figures do nothing at all. They hold no Guard. They can be walked past, walked
over, stood back up, or finished.

**Nothing in this game kills a figure by rolling dice.** Read section 4.

## 2 · THE TURN, AND THE ONE THING YOU GET

### The Guard

At the start of every round, **every standing figure takes one Guard coin.** Put it
where you can see it. That coin is the figure's breath, its balance, its answer, its
readiness to do a thing — all of them, because they are the same thing.

**A Guard is spent to do one of these, at any moment, on anybody's turn:**

- **Turn the blade.** An enemy has just rolled a strike against you. Take away one hit.
- **Meet them.** An enemy walks into your Reach. Strike it the instant it arrives,
  before it can do anything.

**And a Guard is spent to strike.** That is the whole design in one line:

> **Striking spends your Guard. You cannot both hit and answer.**

Every blow you throw opens you. Every answer you make is a blow you will not throw.
A figure that has already Met somebody this round cannot strike on its own
activation, because it has nothing left to strike with. This is not a penalty bolted
onto attacking. It is the same coin.

And it means the oldest trick on the road works: **send someone in first, knowing he
eats the answer, so the blade behind him lands unanswered.**

### A figure's activation

Move, then commit. In that order.

**MOVE** — up to **two hands**. Change facing freely while you move, as much as you
like. Where the ground is bad — climbing, water, a hedge, a bramble bank — you get
**one hand instead of two**. Judged by eye. Coin on a dispute.

Then take **exactly one commitment.** Say it out loud. Everyone can see what you did,
because the Guard coin is either there or it is not.

- **STRIKE** — make one attack. *Spends your Guard.*
- **PUSH ON** — move again, two more hands. *Spends your Guard.*
- **BRACE** — take a Guard coin (you may hold two, never three). While you are braced
  you cannot be given ground.
- **TEND** — reach a friend. Either stand a Down friend up and take one coin off it, or
  take one coin off a standing friend. *Does not spend your Guard.*

You may also do nothing. Nobody minds.

### The round

1. **Breath.** Every standing figure takes a Guard coin. Down figures take none.
2. **Activations.** The side with **fewer standing figures** activates first. Tie: flip
   the coin. Then strictly alternate, one figure each. When a side runs out of figures
   that have not gone, the other side finishes its own.
3. **Light.** Move the round marker. Check the scenario.

No initiative rolls. The side that is losing figures moves first, which is mercy and
also correct.

### Give ground

Some things make a figure **give ground**: move it one hand directly away from
whatever caused it, on ground it could have walked onto. A braced figure never gives
ground. An unconscious figure never gives ground; it is already on the floor.

### Weapons

Three, and there will never be more. They do not give you dice for being better.
They change **where you can fight** and **what your Guard does**.

| | **Where you can strike** | **What it does** |
|---|---|---|
| **SHORT** — knife, hatchet, club, spade, fists, teeth | Reach only (touching, or within a hand) | Take **one more die** when you strike an enemy that has **no Guard** |
| **LONG** — spear, staff, hedging bill, pitchfork, rod | Reach, or one hand beyond it | When you **Meet**, you may instead make them **give ground** and end their move where they stand |
| **THROWN** — bow, sling, stones | Anything you can see | **Cannot strike at Reach.** Too close is too close |

Everyone, with any weapon, can Turn the Blade and can Meet.

### Terrain — what things are

Say what a thing is. Never say what it is for.

- **A wall or a bank.** You are HIGH while you stand on it. It is COVER to anything on
  the far side of it. You cross it with one hand of movement.
- **A hedge.** You cannot see through it. You cross it with one hand of movement.
- **Water.** You cross with one hand of movement, and **you cannot PUSH ON while any
  part of you is in it.** Going Down in water is going Down. Nobody drowns here.
- **A building, a cart, a stack.** You cannot see through it. You cannot walk through it.
- **An ox.** You cannot see through it. It is warm and it does not care.

## 3 · POSITION, AND THE PROOF THAT IT MATTERS

The constitution says the battlefield decides, not the character sheet. That is
either true in the arithmetic or it is a lie in the prose. Here is the arithmetic.

Every die is a coin-flip: a 4, 5 or 6 on a d6, so exactly one hit in two. What
follows is *net wounds actually landed on a Grit 3 figure* — overkill discarded,
because overkill is where a positional advantage would quietly disappear.

| Handful | Hits rolled | **Wounds landed** | Wasted | Chance it drops them | Gain from this die |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | — cannot attack — | 0.000 | — | 0% | — |
| 1 | 0.50 | 0.500 | 0.00 | 0% | +0.500 |
| 2 | 1.00 | 1.000 | 0.00 | 0% | +0.500 |
| 3 | 1.50 | 1.500 | 0.00 | 12.5% | +0.500 |
| 4 | 2.00 | 1.938 | 0.06 | 31.3% | +0.438 |
| 5 | 2.50 | 2.281 | 0.22 | 50.0% | +0.344 |
| 6 | 3.00 | 2.531 | 0.47 | 65.6% | +0.250 |
| 7 | 3.50 | 2.703 | 0.80 | 77.3% | +0.172 |

**The two spreads, measured against each other.**

- **The entire character sheet.** Skill 1 to Skill 3, standing in exactly the same
  place: 0.500 → 1.500 wounds. **The whole sheet is worth 1.000 wounds.**
- **Position, for one ordinary Skill 2 figure.** Worst place you can still attack from
  (one penalty, one die): 0.500. Best place (high, behind them, and they are
  unsupported — five dice): 2.281. **Position is worth 1.781 wounds.**
- With a SHORT weapon against a figure that has already spent its Guard, six dice:
  2.531. **Position is worth 2.031 wounds.**
- And counting the place you cannot attack from at all (two penalties, no handful):
  0 → 2.281. **Position is worth 2.281 wounds.**

**Position is worth between 1.8 and 2.3 times the entire spread of the character
sheet.** Principle 3 holds, with room.

**The substitution test — the one that says it out loud.**

- **Wren, thirteen, Skill 1**, up on the bank, behind them, and they have nobody near
  them: four dice. **1.938 wounds.**
- **Marrow Quist, Skill 2**, the best shot on the road, loosing at something behind a
  wall: one die. **0.500 wounds.**
- **Fisk, Skill 3**, who was a soldier, walking straight at a supported enemy with
  nothing arranged: three dice. **1.500 wounds.**

**The child in the right place hits nearly four times as hard as the grown man in the
wrong one, and out-hits the soldier who has done nothing to earn his ground.** That is
the game.

**Saturation check — Known Risk 4, checked and passed.** The seventh die is still
worth +0.172 wounds. The marginal die never falls to nothing across the whole legal
range, and at the very best handful in the game only 23% of hits are wasted, and
that requires arranging five separate conditions at once. Against Grit 2 figures
waste climbs faster (37% at six dice), which is why Grit 2 belongs to small and
fragile things and why spending your best angle on the weakest enemy is a mistake.
That is a lesson, not a bug.

**Floor check — Known Risk 5, checked and passed.** There is no "never below one die"
rule. A handful of nothing is a handful of nothing, and the attack does not happen.
Four of the fourteen figures in the starter box (29%) have Skill 1; a single penalty
takes them to nothing. That is correct and it is the point — a thirteen-year-old with
a sling genuinely cannot hit a man behind a wall. She has three hands of movement and
she can climb anything for one hand, so the game hands her the answer: **move.**

**What the Guard is worth, and why that shape is right.**

| Attack | Wounds | After one Guard | Guard removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 dice | 1.000 | 0.250 | 75% of it |
| 3 dice | 1.500 | 0.625 | 58% |
| 5 dice | 2.281 | 1.500 | 34% |
| 7 dice | 2.703 | 2.211 | 18% |

A Guard almost cancels a bad blow and barely dents a good one. **Readiness beats a
poor attack; nothing beats good ground.** Which means: do not throw weak strikes at a
figure that still has its coin — take the coin off it first, with somebody you can
afford to send.

## 4 · WOUNDS, DOWN, AND DEATH

**Falling is not dying.** A figure that runs out of Grit falls over. That is all that
has happened. It is not dead, and no roll of any die in this game can kill it.

**Standing over.** A standing figure whose commitment is STRIKE, in Reach of a Down
figure, may do one of two things instead of striking:

- **Finish it.** No roll. It is dead. Write it down.
- **Spare it.** No roll. Nothing happens. Write it down.

The rules will not tell you which of those to do, they will not reward either, and
they will not remember which you chose except in the sentence you write. Nothing here
scores a kill.

> **The age gate is a line, not a system.** Before you start, the table may agree to
> leave **Finish** out entirely. Then Down figures are simply carried off the board by
> whoever reaches them. Play does not change in any other way. *(This is the smallest
> assumption I could make — the handoff lists the age gate as an open question and I
> have not invented an answer to it. See section 10.)*

### After the fight — the Walk Home

Do this once, at the table, before anyone puts anything away. It takes two minutes.

**For each of your figures that was Down when the fight ended, roll one die.**

- **If a standing friend was within a hand of it when the fight ended — roll two dice
  and keep the higher.** Somebody stayed.
- **If there was no standing friend anywhere near it — roll two dice and keep the
  lower.** Nobody did.

| Die | | |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **They do not come home.** | Take the card. Turn it face down. It stays in the stack. |
| **2–3** | **A scar.** | They come home changed. Roll on the Scars. Write the sentence. |
| **4–5** | **Sore.** | They come home. They start the next fight with one coin already on them. |
| **6** | **Up and about.** | They come home. |

What that actually costs:

| | dead | scar | sore | clean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Somebody stayed with them | **2.8%** | 22.2% | 44.4% | 30.6% |
| Nobody was near | **30.6%** | 44.4% | 22.2% | 2.8% |

**Eleven times over.** Whether someone was standing next to your friend when the light
went is worth eleven times more than anything else on this page. Being alone is the
worst thing that can happen to someone, and that is not a slogan here, it is the
number.

**The animals always come home.** A figure whose card says *Animal* is never rolled
for. Ever. It comes home. Write about its limp if you like.

### The Scars

Roll a die. Then **write one sentence on the card in your own words.** The sentence is
the scar. If anyone ever asks at the table what a scar does, **read the sentence** — the
sentence rules. If the sentence does not say, flip the coin.

| | | **It takes** | **It gives** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A leg that does not bend.** | Your Move is one hand. | You can never be given ground. |
| 2 | **A hand that will not close.** | SHORT weapons only, forever. | One more die whenever you Meet. |
| 3 | **Half-deaf.** | You never take the BLIND SIDE die. | Nobody takes the BLIND SIDE die against you either. |
| 4 | **A face people look away from.** | You may not TEND. | The first enemy to enter your Reach each fight loses its Guard. |
| 5 | **Something you saw at the ford.** | You cannot BRACE. | You may PUSH ON without spending your Guard. |
| 6 | **A name people say now.** | Enemy brains always pick you when you are a legal choice. | No friend within a hand of you is ever Alone. |

**The law that keeps this honest:** *a scar's cost applies always; a scar's gift
applies in one named situation.* Every scar is a bad trade and still worth having
survived. If you write your own, write it that way, or the Walk Home stops being a
ladder and starts being a shopping list.

### The Fire

Before each fight, name **two** of your figures who sat by the fire together, and
write one sentence about what they said. **Both of them take two Guard coins in the
first round instead of one.**

Two. Not everyone. You are choosing who got looked after, and that is the point.

Bodies heal with care and time; that is TEND, and the coin you take off. Minds heal
with other people; that is this.

## 5 · THE ENEMY, WHICH RUNS ITSELF

Nobody is the referee. The other side is run off what is printed on its cards, by
whoever feels like reading them, and it never requires a judgement call.

### Three rules, printed once, used everywhere

**Which enemy figure goes.** When it is the enemy's turn to activate, take the enemy
figure **nearest to any of your figures** that has not gone yet. Ties: **flip the coin.**

**Which of your figures it means.** When a brain says *an enemy* and more than one
fits, it means the one with the **most coins on it**. Still tied, the **closest**.
Still tied, **flip the coin.**

**How it walks.** It walks the **shortest way it can walk**. Where two ways look the
same, it takes the one that **ends nearer a friend of its own**. Still the same, **flip
the coin.** It never walks into water when a dry way is no longer. It turns at the end
of its move to face the nearest enemy.

**When it spends its Guard.** The instant it can: to **Turn the Blade** the first time a
strike would put a coin on it, or to **Meet** the first figure that walks into its
Reach — whichever happens first. An enemy holding a LONG weapon always chooses **give
ground** when it Meets.

That is every decision the enemy will ever make. Read the brain from the top and do
the first line that is true. **The bottom line of every brain is always true.**

### The brains

**BRAIN A — the ordinary one.** *(A Tallyman, a Hook)*
1. If an enemy is in your Reach — **STRIKE** it.
2. If an enemy is within two hands — walk into Reach and **STRIKE** it.
3. If you can see an enemy — walk toward the nearest one and **BRACE**.
4. Walk toward the Pull and **BRACE**.

**BRAIN B — the one they send first.** *(A Counter)*
1. If an enemy in your Reach has no Guard — **STRIKE** it.
2. If an enemy is within two hands — walk into Reach and **STRIKE** it. It does not
   matter what happens to you.
3. If you can see an enemy — walk toward the nearest one and **PUSH ON**.
4. Walk toward the Pull and **BRACE**.

**BRAIN C — the one who is keeping accounts.** *(The Reeve)*
1. If two or more of ours are Down — walk toward your own table edge, the shortest
   way, and **BRACE**. He is leaving with what he already has.
2. If an enemy is in your Reach — **STRIKE** it.
3. If an enemy within two hands has no Guard — walk into Reach and **STRIKE** it.
4. If one of ours is in an enemy's Reach — walk to within a hand of that one and **BRACE**.
5. Walk toward the nearest enemy and **BRACE**.

**BRAIN D — the one on the high ground.** *(A Stone-thrower)*
1. If an enemy is in your Reach — walk directly away from it as far as you can, then **BRACE**.
2. If you can see an enemy — **STRIKE** it. Do not move first unless moving is the only
   way to see one.
3. Walk to the highest place you can reach and **BRACE**.

**BRAIN E — the one who buries theirs.** *(Adder Pye)*
1. If one of ours is Down within four hands — walk to it and **TEND**.
2. If **anybody's** figure is Down within four hands — walk to it and **TEND**. He does
   not check whose.
3. If somebody struck you this round — **STRIKE** the nearest enemy.
4. Walk toward the nearest Down figure of anybody's; if there is none, walk away from
   the nearest enemy; and **BRACE**.

Yes. Line 2 means the enemy's gravedigger will walk across the ford and pick your
people up off the ground. He will do it every time. He is not doing it to trick you.

The rules have nothing further to say about him.

### THE DIAL

Agree on one number before you start. Each step contains the one below it, so the
dial only ever goes one way.

1. **A walk.** Enemy figures take no Guard, ever.
2. **Careful.** Enemy figures take a Guard in the first round only.
3. **Even.** Enemy figures take a Guard every round, the same as yours. *(Start here.)*
4. **Sharp.** As Even, and add the one extra figure the scenario names.
5. **Bad ground.** As Even, add that extra figure, and the enemy side always
   activates first.

The dial never touches a die. Every number in section 3 is true at every setting.

## 6 · THE ROAD, AND THE PEOPLE ON IT

> The road runs north-west out of the low country, up over the shoulder of the hills,
> and down again into places that have different words for bread. It is a green road:
> forty feet of turf between two walls, wide enough for beasts to spread and graze as
> they go, and it has been there longer than anything that stands beside it.
>
> A drove is the handful of people who move along it. They walk cattle and geese and
> whatever else needs to be somewhere else by autumn. They put the walls back up. They
> dig out the fords when the fords silt. They bury what needs burying, theirs and
> anybody's, because a thing left on a green road is a thing that stops other people
> using it.
>
> Nobody pays them. The road is not owned. That is the whole argument.
>
> The Tally came over from the far side two summers ago with rods and books and a
> reasonable manner. They are not raiders. They are counting. They count the beasts at
> the ford and they count the walls and they write it all down, and they are patient,
> and every single thing they have written down is true.

not fit on the card it is not a rule.

## 7 · THE FIRST FIGHT — *The Ford at Ellenshaw*

> The water is shin-deep and forty feet across and there is nowhere else to cross for
> a day in either direction. The Tally are on the far bank with their rods out. They
> have not drawn anything. They are counting.
>
> The light goes in about an hour.

**About fifty minutes, including the Walk Home. Dial: 3 (Even).**

### The board

A two-foot square, or a coffee table.

```
            +-------------------------- FAR BANK -------------------------+
            |   ###wall###          [barn]                                |
            |                                                             |
            |          ^bank^                                             |
            |                                                             |
            ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ THE WATER — two hands wide ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
            |                                                             |
            |                            ###wall###        ^bank^         |
            |                                                             |
            +------------------------- NEAR BANK -------------------------+
                                (the Drove comes on here)
```

Lay a folded towel across the middle, about two hands wide, corner to corner or
straight across — either is fine. Put a wall (a belt, a row of books) on each bank so
that neither is open ground. Put a bank or a step on each side. Put a box on the far
bank for the barn. It does not need to be pretty and it does not need to be measured.

### The forces

**The Drove:** Cass, Nan, Wren, Tobe, Beacon. *(Odd, Fisk and Marrow are back down the
road with the rest of the herd.)*

**The Tally:** the Reeve, two Tallymen, a Stone-thrower, Adder Pye.

**The Pull** — the place the enemy walks toward when nothing else is true — **is the ford.**

**If you play at Sharp or Bad ground, the extra figure is a Hook.**

### Deployment

1. **Beacon goes down first**, touching the near edge, anywhere along it.
2. Then the **Tally place all five**, anywhere on the far bank, none of them touching
   the water.
3. Then the **Drove place the other four**, touching the near edge.

You commit your slowest, heaviest, most important figure before you have seen where
the trouble is. That is not an accident.

### What you are doing

You are getting the ox across before the light goes.

### The end

The fight ends at the end of **round six**, or the moment every figure on one side is
Down. Then, in this order:

1. **Is Beacon standing on the far bank?** If no — **THE TALLY HAS ITS COUNT.**
2. **Are two or more Drove figures Down?** If yes — **THE LIGHT GOES.** You got across,
   and it cost you.
3. Otherwise — **THE DROVE HAS COME THROUGH.**

Nothing here counts a kill. You can finish every one of them and still lose the ford.

### Then

1. **The Walk Home.** Roll for every Drove figure that was Down at the end. Two dice,
   keep the higher if a standing friend was within a hand of them; keep the lower if
   there was nobody. Not for Tobe. Not for Beacon. The animals come home.
2. **The sentence.** Somebody says out loud what happened, and one person writes **one
   sentence** on the card of anyone who scarred or anyone who did not come home. One.
   Not a paragraph.
3. **The Fire.** Before you play again, name two figures who sat by the fire, and write
   what they said to each other. They take two Guards in the first round of the next
   fight.

Then put the cards in a stack, face-up for the living, face-down for the ones who did
not come back, and put the stack in the box. **The stack is the campaign.** There is no
other campaign layer and there does not need to be one.

*(The Tally roll nothing. Between fights, replace their losses with the same cards.
They have more people than you do. That is not balance; it is the situation.)*

### How the ford actually plays

Beacon moves one hand. He needs roughly four to cross, and he has six rounds. He will
get there — unless four coins go onto him, or somebody blocks the water. The
Stone-thrower alone puts about one coin a round on him. So: somebody has to stand
between the ox and the sling, and standing between things is what Beacon himself is
for, because he is Cover, and Wren can hide behind eleven hundred pounds of ox and
still shoot over it if she climbs the bank.

Cass can hand her Guard to Beacon and walk on empty. Nan is HIGH the whole time she is
touching a wall, which is most of the near bank, and her bill reaches a hand past
Reach. Nobody can PUSH ON in the water, so the ford is the one place on the board
where everybody moves at walking pace, in the open, in front of each other.

And Adder Pye will wade across the ford, in the middle of all of it, to pick your
people up. You will have to decide about that out loud, with everyone listening.

## 8 · THE ONE PAGE

> **THE GREEN ROAD — everything, on one page**
>
> **THE MEASURE** is a hand — the youngest player's. You never lay it down. You look
> and you say. Disagreement: **flip the coin.**
>
> **THE ROLL.** Take a handful of dice. **4, 5, 6 is a hit. Every hit is a wound.**
> Nothing changes the 4. Nothing is ever added to a die.
>
> **THE HANDFUL.** Start with **SKILL** (1–3).
> **Take a die: HIGH** (feet above theirs) · **BLIND SIDE** (behind their shoulders) ·
> **ALONE** (no friend of theirs within a hand).
> **Put one back: COVER** (something solid in the line) · **FAR** (over four hands).
> **No dice left = no attack.** No floor. Move instead.
>
> **WOUNDS.** A coin on the base. Coins = **GRIT** → the figure goes **DOWN**, lay it on
> its side. Extra wounds are lost. **Nothing on this page kills anybody.**
>
> **THE GUARD.** Every standing figure takes one at the start of every round.
> Spend it to **TURN THE BLADE** (remove one hit from a strike on you) or to **MEET**
> (strike an enemy the instant it enters your Reach).
> **Striking spends it too. You cannot both hit and answer.**
>
> **A TURN.** Move up to **two hands** (bad ground: one), turning freely. Then **one** of:
> **STRIKE** — one attack. *Spends Guard.*
> **PUSH ON** — two more hands. *Spends Guard.*
> **BRACE** — take a Guard (max two). Cannot be given ground.
> **TEND** — stand a Down friend up and take a coin off, or take a coin off a standing
> friend. *Free.*
>
> **A ROUND.** Everyone takes a Guard → side with **fewer standing figures** goes first
> (tie: coin), then alternate → move the round marker.
>
> **WEAPONS.** **SHORT** — Reach only; **one more die** against an enemy with no Guard.
> **LONG** — Reach plus a hand; on a Meet may instead make them **give ground** and stop.
> **THROWN** — anything in sight; **cannot strike at Reach.**
>
> **GIVE GROUND** — one hand directly away, onto ground it could walk on.
>
> **STANDING OVER a Down figure**, as your STRIKE: **FINISH** (no roll, it is dead, write
> it down) or **SPARE** (no roll, write it down). Neither is scored. *May be left out
> before the game starts.*
>
> **THE ENEMY.** Activate the enemy nearest any of yours. Read its brain from the top,
> do the first true line; the last line is always true. *An enemy* means the one with
> the most coins; then the closest; then **coin**. It walks the shortest way; ties go to
> the way ending nearer a friend; then **coin**. It spends its Guard the first moment it
> can. LONG enemies always choose give ground.
>
> **THE WALK HOME.** For each of yours **Down at the end**, roll a die. **A standing friend
> within a hand: roll two, keep the higher. Nobody near: roll two, keep the lower.**
> **1** — does not come home · **2–3** — a scar, write the sentence · **4–5** — sore, one coin
> next fight · **6** — up and about.
> **Animals are never rolled for. They come home.**
>
> **THE FIRE.** Before each fight name **two** figures who sat together and write one
> sentence. Both take **two Guards** in round one.

## 9 · DESIGN LOG

Every mechanic below is here because a principle forced it. Where nothing forced it,
it is not in the game.

**One roll, target 4, dice as the only currency.**
*Forced by:* "No number is ever added to a die." "Judged by eye, settled in seconds."
"A rule is wrong if it fails the youngest player or the clock."
*Rejected:* roll-and-keep-highest (position saturates fast — the sixth die is worth
almost nothing on a keep-highest ladder, which is exactly Known Risk 4). Opposed
rolls (doubles the dice and the time on every single attack). Any target number that
moves (it is a number added to a die wearing a hat).

**Five position questions, three up and two down.**
*Forced by:* "The battlefield decides, not the character sheet." "A rule you cannot see
on the table is not a rule." Every question is answered by looking at the table, and
none by looking at a card. Computed in section 3: worth 1.8–2.3× the whole sheet.
*Rejected:* a cover *value* (1 die / 2 dice) — it needs a table, and Value 6 forbids
depth from a table of combinations. Range bands finer than "close / seen / far" — they
need a tape measure and the tape measure needs a minute.

**No handful = no attack, instead of a one-die floor.**
*Forced by:* Known Risk 5 directly, and by "deny it the geometry, not a budget." A floor
would make COVER a no-op for 29% of the roster. Instead the geometry says no, which is
what the DNA asks for by name.

**One hit, one wound, surplus lost.**
*Forced by:* Known Risk 6. Any ladder where a bigger result does something *different*
can be non-monotonic, and I could not write one that wasn't. One-to-one is monotone by
construction and cannot be got wrong.
*Rejected:* a spend-your-hits menu (push / pin / wound). It was the most interesting
thing I designed and it took forty seconds an attack and it produced two orderings that
crossed. Cut on the clock and on Risk 6.

**The Guard — one coin, and striking spends it.**
*Forced by:* "Committing forecloses something," which the handoff names as the thesis
and as a test. This is the whole game's answer to it: your blow and your answer are the
same coin, so every attack opens you and every parry is an attack you gave up. It is
also the direct answer to the memory the handoff promises — *sending a helper in first,
knowing he eats the answer, so the blade behind him lands unanswered.* That memory is
only reachable if the answer is limited, so I limited it. **That is an assumption on
Open Question 8 and it is flagged in section 10.**
*Rejected:* an action-point economy (it is a budget, and Principle 3 says deny the
geometry, not the budget). A tempo penalty for melee — attack and your next turn is
shorter — which is Open Question 6 and was parked pending a table test I cannot run.

**BRACE, and the second Guard.**
*Forced by:* Risk 7. Without it, STRIKE dominates every activation and half the design
never fires. BRACE makes "I did not attack" into a real move with a real payoff, so the
foreclosure cuts both ways.

**Three weapons that trade instead of scaling.**
*Forced by:* Risk 7. If a weapon only gives dice, one weapon is strictly worse and
everything hanging off it silently disappears. SHORT punishes a spent Guard; LONG
denies the run-up; THROWN reaches anything but cannot fight in your face. Checked
pairwise: none dominates.
*Rejected:* damage values on weapons (a number, and Grit is only 2–4, so any weapon
doing 2 would delete position by overkill — Risk 4 again).

**Facing that only turns on your own activation.**
*Forced by:* "Committing forecloses something" and "a rule you cannot see." Where a
figure is pointed is visible from across the room and it is a decision you have to live
with until your next turn.

**Falling is not dying; death only from a choice.**
*Forced by:* the non-negotiable, and Emotional Goal 2 — *a death should be a decision
someone made, not a number that came up.* So no number can produce one. FINISH and
SPARE are the only two doors, they take no roll, and the rules refuse to grade either.
*Rejected:* a wound table with a death result. It makes death a number.

**The Walk Home — one die, and who was standing next to them.**
*Forced by:* False Constraint 3 (one die roll and one written sentence is the only
persistence that has survived), DNA 9, and Emotional Goal 1. The keep-higher /
keep-lower swing is the arithmetic of DNA 9: **2.8% against 30.6%.** Nothing else in the
game moves a number eleven-fold.
*Rejected:* a recovery track, an injury chart with locations, anything needing a second
page. Also rejected: a modifier. Two dice and pick one is not a modifier and it is
faster.

**Scars: broad cost, narrow gift.**
*Forced by:* Emotional Goal 6 — a veteran should be visibly changed, not merely worse —
against Risk 6, because a scar that is a net gain makes the Walk Home ladder
non-monotonic. The stated law (*cost always, gift in one named situation*) is the lint
rule that keeps it ordered, and it is printed for anyone writing their own.

**The sentence is the mechanic.**
*Forced by:* Emotional Goal 5 and DNA 1 — the table is the memory. The written line on
the card is the authority on what a scar does. Nothing persistent lives in a head or in
software.

**The Fire.**
*Forced by:* DNA 9 — *minds heal with other people* — and Risk 8, which says the
emotional layer keeps being scheduled and never built. It is two figures, one sentence,
and one extra coin each, and it is built.

**Brains: numbered lines, plain English, last line always true, coin at the bottom.**
*Forced by:* "Nobody at the table is the referee" and False Constraint 7 — plain English
did the job in fewer words with no collisions, so there is no symbol grammar. The three
universal rules (who goes, who it means, how it walks) exist so that no brain ever has
to contain a judgement call.

**Adder Pye's line 2.**
*Forced by:* DNA 8 — *the enemy is a person, and killing is a choice the rules refuse to
score* — and Emotional Goal 7 — *the room should have to argue, out loud, about
something the rules refuse to settle.* He tends anyone's fallen, including yours,
every time, forever. The rules then say nothing else about him at all.

**The Dial in five containing steps, none of which touch a die.**
*Forced by:* Emotional Goal 8 and the two judges. It is monotone by containment, it is
visible on the table (coins present or absent, one more figure, who goes first), and
because it never touches the dice, every number in section 3 holds at every setting.

**Beacon is Cover. Nan is HIGH on a wall. Wren climbs.**
*Forced by:* DNA 10 — *say what a thing is; never say what it is for.* An ox blocks
sight. It does not "grant the Cover keyword to allied models." The rules describe the
ox; the players discover the tactic.

**What is deliberately switched off, with its sockets showing.** *(False Constraint 10
says this is allowed.)* Recruitment. Trade and provisions. Anything about why the Tally
came over the hills. A campaign-level way to lose everything. The Tally's own Walk
Home. Each of those has a visible hook — the card stack, the Fire's blank second half,
the enemy cards you replace between fights — and none of them has machinery, because
the game is complete without them and Risk 3 says architecture crowds out content.

## 10 · THE TEN RISKS, CHECKED

**1 · The rebuild reflex.** One game, one vocabulary, built once. The vocabulary is not
elegant — *Guard* does four jobs, *hand* does three — and I did not go back to fix it,
because Risk 1 says that is the trap.

**2 · Nothing has ever been played.** Every number here is a hypothesis. **In order, the
first three to change at a real table:** (a) Grit 3 — if fights drag, drop the standard
figure to Grit 2 and accept the extra overkill; (b) six rounds at the ford — likeliest
to be one round too many; (c) whether BRACE giving one Guard is enough to compete with
STRIKE. Do not touch the target number, and do not add a floor.

**3 · Architecture crowds out content.** Sections 6, 7 and the brains — the setting, the
fourteen figures, the scenario — are the largest part of this document. There is no
governance section, no schema, no glossary, no versioning, no custodian.

**4 · Position saturates. CHECKED — passed.** Section 3. The seventh die still buys
+0.172 wounds; the marginal die never approaches zero. Position is worth 1.8–2.3× the
whole character sheet, and the weakest figure on the roster, well placed,
out-hits the best shot on it, badly placed, by 3.9×. Waste at the best realistic handful is 8.8%.

**5 · Floors delete penalties. CHECKED — passed.** There is no floor. Zero dice is zero
dice and the attack does not happen. 29% of the roster rolls one die and a single
penalty stops them attacking — checked, intended, and each of those figures has movement
or a card line that lets them fix it by walking somewhere else.

**6 · Non-monotonic bands. CHECKED — passed, and it changed two things.** Hits are wounds
one-for-one, so the attack ladder is monotone by construction; a spend-your-hits menu
was cut for exactly this. The Walk Home is ordered by permanence (gone → marked forever
→ marked once → clean) and the scar law (*cost always, gift once*) is what keeps a 2
from being better than a 4. The Dial is monotone by containment. Those are the only
three ladders in the game.

**7 · Dominated options. CHECKED — passed.** Weapons checked pairwise (SHORT's guardless
die vs LONG's reach and run-up denial vs THROWN's range) — none dominates. Commitments
checked (STRIKE / PUSH ON / BRACE / TEND) — each does something the others cannot, and
BRACE exists specifically so STRIKE does not dominate. One deliberate near-domination
remains: a one- or two-die strike into a fresh Guard is almost worthless (it removes
75–100% of the output). That is intentional, it has named counterplay — spend the
Guard with somebody cheap first — and it is the engine of the memory the handoff asks
for.

**8 · The emotional layer.** Built, not scheduled: the Walk Home, the sentence, the
scars, the Fire, ALONE on both layers, FINISH and SPARE, the animals coming home, and
Adder Pye. Not built and not pretended: recruitment, and any accounting of mercy — the
constitution forbids a procedure for what a person is worth, so mercy is a choice with
no number attached and never will have one.

**9 · Tone dies in schema.** There is fiction in section 6, a line of it on every card,
and prose in the scenario. No card says "grants."

**10 · A guard inside the thing it guards.** I have not built a continuity mechanism,
because the handoff says every one of them was abandoned by the next rebuild. What
survives instead is the stack of index cards, which is not part of the rules and does
not care if the rules are rewritten. If someone rebuilds this engine tomorrow, Wren's
scar sentence is still true.

## 11 · WHAT I ASSUMED, AND WHAT I DID NOT ANSWER

The handoff's Open Questions are not answered here. Where play could not continue
without something, I made the smallest assumption I could and named it.

| | Question | What I did |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Has any version been played? | Not answerable. Section 10.2 lists what to change first. |
| **2** | Which prior design line is the product? | Not answerable and not needed. This is one game. |
| **3** | What happened in the nine-day gap? | Not answerable. |
| **4** | Campaign layer: three pages or software? | **Assumed: neither.** It is a stack of index cards, one die and one sentence — False Constraint 3 says that is the only persistence that has survived. No pages, no software. |
| **5** | What is the age gate? | **Assumed the smallest thing:** one printed line letting the table leave FINISH out before starting. Not a rating, not a deck filter, not fiction. If the real answer is one of those, it replaces one line and nothing else. |
| **6** | Is a melee tempo penalty survivable? | **Not built.** It needs a table, not reasoning. The Guard is a *positional* cost for striking, not a tempo cost. If a table finds it too soft, the tempo version is the first thing to try. |
| **7** | Does a mutual charge break run-up denial? | **Assumed the simplest thing:** if both figures Meet, both resolve at the same moment and wounds apply after. LONG's give-ground and a strike happen together. Whether this is right needs a table. |
| **8** | What spends the enemy's one answer, now the answer is unlimited? | **Assumed the answer is limited again** — one Guard, per figure, per round. That is the load-bearing assumption in the whole design, and it is what makes cheap bodies mean something. If the answer must be unlimited, the Guard is what breaks. |
| **9** | Is there a losing condition? | A **scenario** can be lost, and the ford can be lost outright. A **campaign** losing condition is left blank on purpose. |
| **10** | Who else is at this table, and do they want to play? | Not answerable by me. It is the only question here that decides anything.
