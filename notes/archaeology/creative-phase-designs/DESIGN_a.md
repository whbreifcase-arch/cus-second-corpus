# THE GREEN ROAD

*A game about taking people and animals somewhere, and what it costs.*

For two to four people, on a coffee table, in about an hour.
You will need: a dozen figures of any kind, a handful of six-sided dice, a pocketful
of coins, two strips of card, a pencil, and a sheet of paper you are willing to keep.

---

> The water at Rhyd-y-gors is shallow for the width of a cart and deep either side of
> that, and the men who want your beasts have always known it.
>
> Mared has crossed it forty times. Twice she has crossed it running. Once she crossed
> it carrying Hen Sam's brother, who was heavier than he looked and who did not wake up
> on the far bank.
>
> The beasts do not know any of this. The beasts want the grass they can see.

---

## 0 · HOW TO READ THIS

This is the whole game. Sections 1 to 7 are the rules and the content — read them once
and play. Section 8 is the page you keep on the table. Section 9 is the design log,
which is for the designer and not for the table.

**Two sticks.** Cut two strips of card.
- **The short stick** — two inches. A AA battery is exactly this.
- **The long stick** — six inches. A dollar bill is close enough. So is a pencil.

**Two judges.** The youngest person at the table, and the clock. If a rule is too slow
or too confusing for either of them, that rule is wrong. Simplify it where you sit and
write down what you did. You are not doing it wrong; you are doing the only thing this
game has ever asked for.

**Judged by eye.** Every distance in this game is a look, not a measurement. If it
looks like he can reach, he can reach. Use a stick only when somebody doubts it. If
they still doubt it after the stick, flip a coin and get on with it. Five minutes on
one figure is a fault, not care.

## 1 · THE ROLL

There is one roll in this game, and it never changes.

> **Take one die. That is you.**
>
> **Take one more die for each of these that is true:**
> - **REACH** — you are close enough to strike him with what you carry.
> - **ABOVE** — you are standing on something higher than he is.
> - **CAUGHT** — a friend of yours is also in reach of him.
> - **STILL** — you did not move at all this activation.
>
> **Take one die away for each of these that is true:**
> - **COVER** — something solid is between you and him.
> - **HURT** — there are coins on you.
>
> **Roll them all. Every 4, 5 and 6 is a coin. Put those coins on him.**

That is the entire engine. Nothing is ever added to a die. There is one number to
remember for the rest of your life and it is four.

**There are only four reasons, so you will never roll more than five dice.** That is a
law, not a coincidence — see the audit in §9.

**There is no floor.** If the dice run out, you have no dice, and you cannot do it. A
figure with no dice does not roll and misses. You will know this before you commit,
because you can see the table. Knowing it is the point: the answer to *I have no dice*
is not *roll anyway*, it is *move*.

### What the six words mean, in plain sight

**REACH.** For a hand weapon, your base is touching his. For a long weapon, he is
within a short stick — but **not** if he is touching you; you are inside the spear then
and you do not get the die. For a sling, he is within a long stick. A bow never claims
Reach.

**ABOVE.** You are standing on a thing that clearly lifts you over him — a bank, a
wall, a wagon bed, a table. If the room has to argue about whether it is higher, it is
not higher.

**CAUGHT.** Another friend of yours is also in reach of him, right now. Both of you
claim it. This is the reason to send someone in first.

**STILL.** You did not move so much as a hair this activation. Moving one inch costs
you this die. It is the most expensive die in the game and the cheapest to claim.

**COVER.** Look from his head to yours. If you would be striking or shooting through
something solid, that is Cover. If it only clips the edge of him, it is not. Cover is
one die, once, however much stone there is.

**HURT.** Any coins at all on your base, one die. Two coins do not cost two dice.

### Damage

Coins on the base. Nothing else.

Every person has **GRIT** — 2 or 3. It is printed on their card. When the coins on a
figure equal its Grit, it goes **DOWN**.

> **DOWN.** Lay the figure on its side. Take its coins off. It does nothing for the
> rest of the fight, it cannot be attacked, it cannot get up, and it is not dead.
>
> **If it was ALONE when it fell, lay it face down instead of on its side.** Leave it
> that way. That is the whole record and you will need it after the fight.

Coins past a figure's Grit are simply lost. There is no overkill in this game.

### Weapons

A weapon is not a bonus. It is a shape. Each one says what it is.

| | **Reaches** | **Claims** | **When it lands two or more** | **Will not** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hand weapon** — blade, axe, club | touching | all four | **Shove**: push him a short stick straight back, or step a short stick past him | reach anything not touching |
| **Long weapon** — spear, bill, drover's stick | a short stick | all four | **Stop him**: if you struck him mid-move, his move ends where he stands | claim Reach against anyone actually touching you |
| **Bow** | three long sticks | Above, Still | — | claim Reach or Caught; be used at all with an enemy within a short stick; and past **two** long sticks it loses a die |
| **Sling, thrown stone** | one long stick | Reach, Above, Still | — | claim Caught; reach past a long stick |
| **Teeth** (a dog) | touching | Reach, Caught | — | claim Above or Still. Dogs do not stand still |
| **Bare hands** | touching | all four | — | **put the last coin on. You cannot put a person Down with your hands.** |

Bare hands wear a person out. They do not fell them. That is a statement about what
hands are, and it is why an unarmed helper is worth bringing.

## 2 · THE TURN AND THE ROUND

### The round

1. Flip a coin. The winner says who activates first.
2. Sides alternate, **one figure at a time**. When one side has nobody left
   unactivated, the other side activates all the rest.
3. At the end of the round: loose beasts drift (§6). Sweep the stance coins off.
4. Six rounds. Then the light goes and the fight is over.

### The activation — and the door it shuts

When you activate a figure you choose **one** of three things. This is the commitment.
It is not a modifier. Each of the three takes the other two off the table, and it stays
taken until that figure activates again — which means it stays taken *through the other
side's turns*.

> **RUN** — move up to two long sticks. Nothing else. No blow, no shot, no help.
>
> **STAND** — move up to one long stick, or not at all, and then do **one** thing: a
> blow, a shot, a Help, pick a friend up, put a friend down, or Finish. If you did not
> move at all, claim the **Still** die.
>
> **WAIT** — do not move and do nothing now. Instead you hold a blow in your hand. At
> any moment before your next activation — including in the middle of an enemy's move —
> say **"now"** before any die is rolled, and take your one thing then, claiming the
> **Still** die.

**Mark it.** Put a coin *beside* the figure — not on it. Heads up means it is WAITing
and still has a blow in hand. Tails up means it has acted and has nothing left. No coin
means it has not gone yet this round. When a WAITing figure spends its blow, flip the
coin to tails. Flipping the coin *is* spending it.

Coins **on** the base are hurts. The coin **beside** the figure is what it is doing.

### What each one forecloses

- **You RAN.** You crossed ground. You have no blow in hand when they come, and you are
  standing exactly where the run left you, which is usually in the open. You live with
  that until you go again.
- **You STOOD.** You did one thing. You did not cover ground and you have nothing held
  back.
- **You WAITED.** You have a blow ready at a moment of your choosing, with the Still
  die guaranteed. You went nowhere. The beasts moved without you. Somebody crossed the
  water while you watched the ford.

A drover who fights is a drover who is not droving. That is the whole game and it is in
the activation, not in a table.

**Two Waits at once.** If both sides want to spend a Wait at the same instant, flip.

### The things you can do on a STAND

**A blow or a shot.** The roll in §1.

**Help.** Touch a friend and do nothing else. Until your next activation, that friend
claims the **Still** die whether it moved or not, and it is not Alone. This is what
people without weapons are for, and it is not a small thing: it is the fourth die.

**Carry.** Touch a Down friend and pick it up. While carrying you move a short stick on
a STAND, you cannot RUN, you cannot strike, you cannot Help. Put them down as your one
thing.

**Finish.** See §4. Read the box before you use it.

### Being Alone

> **A figure is ALONE if no friend of its side is within a short stick of it.**

Look at the table. That is the whole test.

- An Alone figure cannot be Helped and cannot claim **Caught**. Both of those are
  simply true; no rule is needed.
- **An Alone figure with coins on it has no choices left.** On its activation it must
  either move toward the nearest friend of its side, or WAIT. It may not go anywhere
  else and it may not attack unless something walks into the Wait.
- **If it goes Down while Alone, it goes down face first**, and it loses a die after
  the fight.

Being alone does not make you weaker. It takes your choices away. That is worse.

## 3 · POSITION, AND WHY IT IS THE LANGUAGE

### The Law of the Board

> **Stand anywhere on the table. If from that spot you cannot get to something solid or
> something high within one long stick, there is not enough terrain. Put more on before
> you start.**

A 24-inch board wants at least four solid things and two high things. Books under a
cloth. A cereal box. A phone stood on its edge is a wall. This law is not decoration —
it is the only thing that makes the rest of §3 true. Play on a bare table and this game
is a coin flip.

### The payoff, with the working shown

The handoff warns that position saturates: when damage is a small integer and health
pools are small, extra dice stop being worth anything. So here is the arithmetic,
before the law and not after it.

A hit is a 4, 5 or 6 — half the die. Coins accumulate; they do not reset. So a pool of
*n* dice puts on *n*/2 coins per attack, and a figure of Grit *g* goes Down after
*g* ÷ (*n*/2) attacks.

| pool | avg coins | chance of ≥1 | ≥2 | ≥3 | attacks to Down a Grit 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.00 | — | — | — | never |
| 1 | 0.50 | 50.0% | 0% | 0% | 6.00 |
| 2 | 1.00 | 75.0% | 25.0% | 0% | 3.00 |
| 3 | 1.50 | 87.5% | 50.0% | 12.5% | 2.00 |
| 4 | 2.00 | 93.8% | 68.8% | 31.3% | 1.50 |
| 5 | 2.50 | 96.9% | 81.3% | 50.0% | 1.20 |

**Does it saturate?** Saturation would mean the top of the ladder buys nothing. The
biggest pool in the game is 5 and puts on 2.50 coins. A person's Grit is 3. **2.50 is
under 3**, so no attack in this game is ever certain, and every die added still buys a
full half-coin all the way to the top. In chance-of-felling terms against Grit 3 the
last two dice are worth **+18.8% and +18.8%** — dead straight, no curve, no clipping.

Grit 2 figures do saturate slightly: their last two dice are worth +18.8% and +12.5%.
That is why Grit 2 is rare in the roster and mostly belongs to animals and people who
should not be in the fight at all.

**Is position worth more than the whole character sheet?** This is the test the handoff
sets, so here it is both ways.

*The entire spread of the character sheet*, holding position fixed at a good spot where
all four reasons are available:

- Best figure the sheet can produce: an armed person, 5 dice, against the frailest
  target in the roster (Grit 2) → **0.80 attacks to Down**.
- Worst figure the sheet can produce: Cadi the dog, whose teeth never claim Above or
  Still, so 3 dice, against the toughest target in the roster (Grit 3) → **2.00
  attacks to Down**.
- **Total sheet swing: 2.50×.** That is everything — every trait, every weapon, every
  Grit value, best against worst, all at once.

*The spread of position*, holding the sheet fixed at one ordinary person attacking one
ordinary Grit 3 person:

- Standing over him on a bank, in reach, with a friend on him, having not moved: 5
  dice → **1.20 attacks**.
- Standing in the open having just run to him: 1 die → **6.00 attacks**.
- **Position swing: 5.00×.**
- And below that, position can produce **0 dice — you cannot attack at all**. No
  character sheet in this game can do that to anybody.

**Position is worth exactly twice the entire spread of the character sheet, and it has a
floor the sheet cannot reach.** Principle 3 holds, and it holds by construction: the
sheet's ceiling and the board's ceiling are the same number, five, but the board's
floor is zero and the sheet's floor is one.

There is a second consequence worth saying out loud. **You cannot fell a healthy person
in one blow with fewer than three dice.** Walking up to someone and hitting them is two
dice. It cannot kill. Everything lethal in this game is arranged beforehand.

## 4 · HURT, DOWN, HOME, AND DEAD

Falling is not dying. Nothing in a fight kills anybody.

A fight puts coins on people and knocks people Down. That is all it does. Death happens
in exactly two places, and both of them are outside the roll:

1. Someone decides to do it.
2. The night after, when you find out who was with them.

### The Finish

> **THE FINISH — an older table's rule. Leave it out and nothing else changes.**
>
> A figure touching a Down enemy may, as its one thing on a STAND, kill them. There is
> no roll. Take the figure off the table. Cross it off its card. The player who did it
> writes the line.
>
> It costs you a whole activation. It gains you nothing. No scenario in this book asks
> for it, no result in this book counts it, and no other rule anywhere refers to it.
> The game will not tell you whether to do it.
>
> *Nothing here is written to make this an interesting decision. It is written to make
> it a real one.*

If your table does not want this rule, take it out. Nothing hangs off it. If your table
does want it, note that the enemy only ever uses it at the top notch of the dial (§5),
which is a thing you set on purpose, in front of everyone.

### After the fight — one die and one sentence

Do not tidy the table. The table is still holding the answer.

**For every figure lying Down — yours and theirs both — count its dice:**

- **One die, always.**
- **One more** if a friend of its side was touching it when the fight ended.
- **One more** if a friend carried it off the board.
- **One more** if it was **not** Alone when it fell — that is, if it is lying on its
  side and not on its face.

**Roll them all and keep the highest.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **They do not come home.** Cross them off. Write the line. |
| **2** | A **scar**, rolled — and they are not fit for the next fight. |
| **3** | A **scar**, rolled. |
| **4–5** | A **scar of your choosing.** Roll for it instead if you would rather. |
| **6** | **No scar.** If you would rather have one, take one of your choosing. |

Each band contains the one below it, on purpose, so a better roll can never hand you a
worse outcome. See §9.

**The chances, so you know what you are choosing.** With one die, one in six do not
come home. With two — someone stayed with them — it is one in thirty-six. With three,
one in two hundred and sixteen.

Somebody sitting down next to a fallen figure and not moving turns a one-in-six into a
one-in-thirty-six. That is the largest number in this entire game, and it is bought
with no roll, no stat, and no weapon. **Just being there.**

Nobody came, and they fell alone, and nobody carried them: one die. One in six. That is
a decision somebody made.

### The scars

Roll a die, or choose. Write it on the card, in ink, forever.

Every scar takes something and gives something, because that is what surviving does.

**1 · A leg that sets slow.**
*Takes:* you cannot RUN.
*Gives:* when you STAND, you claim the **Still** die even if you moved.

**2 · A hand that will not close.**
*Takes:* you cannot carry a long weapon or a bow.
*Gives:* you may Help a friend and strike, both, in the same activation.

**3 · You went under and came up.**
*Takes:* you cannot be Helped. You do not let people near you now.
*Gives:* you do not lose the die for being **Hurt**.

**4 · You will not be caught out twice.**
*Takes:* you may never RUN toward an enemy — only away, or across.
*Gives:* on a WAIT you may move a short stick before you strike.

**5 · You saw it happen.** ✱
*Takes:* you cannot claim the **Still** die while you are Alone.
*Gives:* while any friend is within a short stick, you claim the **Still** die whether
you moved or not.

**6 · You stayed when you should have run.**
*Takes:* when a friend goes Down within a long stick of you, your next activation must
be a STAND or a WAIT that does not take you further from them.
*Gives:* a Down friend you are touching at the end of a fight gets **two** dice for you
instead of one.

**✱ is a scar of the mind.** Bodies heal with care and time. Minds heal with other
people. Between fights you may write one line naming two figures who spent that time
together — where they were, what was said. If you do, one of them may rub out one ✱
scar. It is gone. Nothing else in this game comes off a card.

### The line, and the Road Book

For every figure that went Down, write **one sentence** on its card. The sentence has
to name something that was actually on the table — the wall, the water, who was
standing there.

Then write **one sentence** in the Road Book, which is a single sheet of paper: where
you were, how many got across, and who did not come home.

That is the entire campaign. One die and one sentence. It does not need a computer and
it does not need a second book.

**Reading the road.** Before a fight, one figure may read one line from its own card
out loud to the table. On its first roll that fight, it claims the **Still** die,
whatever it did. A figure with nothing written on it has nothing to read.

**Rolling for the other side too.** Roll for the Bailiff's men exactly as you roll for
yours, and write their lines in the Road Book beside yours. They are people. They come
back to the next fight carrying what happened at this one. If you never do this, the
enemy is scenery, and this game does not have scenery.

## 5 · THE BAILIFF'S MEN, WHO RUN THEMSELVES

Nobody at this table is the referee. One player is **the Reader**. The Reader is on
nobody's side; the Reader reads. The Reader does not decide, does not plan, does not
choose a good move. If the list says a stupid thing, the Reader does the stupid thing.
The Reader is the weather.

### How to read them

- Activate them in printed order: **Ruthin, Idris, Twm, Tal, Enoch.**
- On a figure's activation, read its list from the top. **Do the first line that is
  true, then stop.**
- "Nearest" is judged by eye. **If two are the same, flip a coin.** There is a coin at
  the bottom of everything.
- If a line says go somewhere you cannot reach — go as far as you can toward it. That
  is a RUN.
- If a line says strike and you turn out not to be able to, go on to the next line.
- **If a figure is Alone and has coins on it**, ignore its list: it moves toward the
  nearest of its own and that is a RUN. If it has none left, it WAITs.
- **No Bailiff's man ever attacks a beast**, whatever a line says.
- The Bailiff's men never Finish anybody unless the dial is at three.

Every list ends in a line that always works. You will never be stuck.

---

**BAILIFF RUTHIN** — Grit 3. A sword. *He wants the beasts, not the fight.*

1. If a beast is touching you — **lead it**: STAND, move a short stick toward the **east
   edge**, and the beast comes with you.
2. If a beast is within two long sticks and no drove figure is within a short stick of
   it — go to it. RUN.
3. If a drove figure is in your reach — strike it. Strike the one with the most coins
   on it.
4. If a drove figure is within a long stick — go to it and strike. STAND.
5. Go toward the nearest beast. RUN.

**IDRIS** — Grit 3. A bill (long weapon). *He waits.*

1. If a drove figure is in your reach — strike the one with the most coins on it.
2. If a drove figure is within two long sticks — WAIT.
3. Go toward the nearest drove figure. RUN.

**TWM** — Grit 3. A club. *He goes for whoever is bleeding.*

1. If a drove figure with coins on it is in your reach — strike it.
2. If any drove figure is in your reach — strike it.
3. If a drove figure with coins on it is within two long sticks — go to it, and strike
   if you get there. STAND if you reach, RUN if you do not.
4. Go toward the nearest drove figure. RUN.

**TAL** — Grit 2. A bow. *He will not shoot into a crowd.*

1. If a drove figure is within a short stick of you — move a long stick directly away
   from it. RUN.
2. If you can see a drove figure within three long sticks that is **not** in reach of
   one of your own and **not** in cover — do not move, and shoot the nearest of them.
3. If you can see a drove figure within three long sticks that is not in reach of one
   of your own — do not move, and shoot the nearest of them.
4. Go to the highest thing within a long stick that you could see a drove figure from.
   RUN. If there is no such thing, go toward the nearest drove figure. RUN.

**ENOCH, WHO DIGS** — Grit 3. A spade, and he does not swing it. *He will not strike
anyone, ever.*

1. If you are touching a figure that is Down — do nothing at all.
2. Go to the nearest Down figure, of either side. RUN.
3. Go toward the nearest of your own. RUN.

> **A Down figure that Enoch is touching when the fight ends counts as having had
> someone stay with it. Including yours.**
>
> Enoch is unarmed. He will never attack anybody. He is worth nothing to any victory
> condition on either side. If you kill him, no rule in this book changes, and the next
> figure of yours that falls near him will roll one die instead of two.
>
> The rules have nothing further to say about this. That is deliberate. Talk about it.

### The dial

One token on the Bailiff's card. Set it out loud, before deployment, where everyone can
see it. Move it a notch if the last fight was the wrong shape.

- **ONE — WARY.** The lists exactly as printed.
- **TWO — PRESSING.** Put this line at the top of every list except Enoch's:
  *If a drove figure is Alone and within two long sticks — go to it, and strike if you
  get there.*
- **THREE — MERCILESS.** Keep the Pressing line, and put this one above it:
  *If a Down drove figure is in your reach and no drove figure is touching it — Finish
  it.*

Notch three turns the darkest rule in the game on for the other side. It is a switch
you throw in front of the whole table, in daylight, and anyone at the table can ask you
not to.

If a fight comes out one-sided in numbers rather than in behaviour, take Twm off the
board, or add a second Twm. That is the other dial and it is allowed.

## 6 · THE DROVE

**GRIT** is 2 or 3. **Everyone moves** one long stick on a STAND and two on a RUN,
unless their line says otherwise.

### Mared's road

**MARED VAUGHAN, who has the road** — Grit 3 — a drover's stick (long weapon)
*The beasts know her. Beasts within a long stick of her move with her, not just those
within a short stick.*

**GWILYM PEN-Y-BONT** — Grit 3 — a hedging axe (hand weapon)
*He is slow to move and hard to move. He cannot RUN. He does not lose the die for being
Hurt.*

**NEST, who came with the drove last year** — Grit 3 — a bow
*She shoots at what is quiet. She claims the Still die on a shot even if she moved, as
long as no enemy is within a long stick of her.*

**OWAIN, Mared's son** — Grit 2 — a sling
*He is twelve. He RUNs three long sticks. He cannot make a blow — only a throw.*

**HEN SAM** — Grit 2 — nothing but his hands
*He has buried more people than he has fought. He may Help and still move a long stick
in the same activation, and he carries a Down friend a long stick instead of a short
one.*

**CADI, a dog** — Grit 2 — teeth
*She RUNs three long sticks. No figure within a short stick of Cadi is Alone, and Cadi
is never Alone. A beast within a short stick of Cadi does not drift. Cadi cannot be
Finished, and on her roll after the fight a 1 is read as a 2.*

### The beasts

**THREE HEAD OF CATTLE** — no Grit, no weapon, no card.

- **A beast cannot be attacked. Not by anyone, for any reason, ever.** No animal is
  killed in this game. They come home. They may not come home with *you*.
- **Droving.** Any beast within a short stick of a drove figure when that figure begins
  its activation moves with it, and must end within a short stick of it. This costs
  nothing and is not a choice — it just happens. A figure on a RUN takes the herd two
  long sticks. A figure that stops to fight leaves them where they stand.
- **Leading.** A Bailiff's man touching a beast may lead it a short stick as his one
  thing on a STAND. Slowly. You will have time to get there.
- **Drift.** At the end of each round, every beast with no figure of either side within
  a short stick of it moves **one short stick directly away from the nearest figure on
  the table.** Flip for ties. Cattle scatter from people. That is what they are.

## 7 · THE FORD — the first fight

*Rhyd-y-gors. The water is shallow for the width of a cart and deep either side of
that. Ruthin has a paper in his coat and three men with him and he has stood in the
shallow place since before it was light.*

### The board

Twenty-four inches square. A coffee table.

- **The water** runs across the middle, east to west, two short sticks wide. A blue
  scarf, a belt, two strips of paper.
- **The ford** is one long stick of that water, near the middle. Mark both ends of it
  with anything. **The water can only be crossed at the ford.** Everywhere else it is
  deep and nobody goes in.
- **The bank.** The ground on the north side of the ford is higher. A figure within a
  short stick of the water on the **north** side counts as **Above** anything in the
  water or on the south side within a long stick. Everyone should be able to see this
  by looking; if not, put a book under the north bank.
- **The rest.** Obey the Law of the Board. As a minimum: a stone wall along part of the
  north bank; a thorn hedge in the south half; a ruined shed east of the ford; a fallen
  cart west of the ford, which is a high thing you can stand on. Use whatever is in the
  house.

The drove comes from the **south** and wants the **north**. The Bailiff's men take
beasts off the **east**.

### Deployment

Place them in this order, no arguing:

1. **The drove** — anywhere within a long stick of the south edge. Each of the three
   beasts must start within a short stick of a drove figure.
2. **Idris** — at the north lip of the ford, touching the water.
3. **Ruthin** — one long stick east of Idris.
4. **Twm** — one long stick west of Idris.
5. **Tal** — on the highest thing north of the water. If there is none, on the north
   bank one long stick east of Ruthin.
6. **Enoch** — touching the north edge, behind Ruthin.

Set the dial. Flip for first round.

### When it ends

At the end of round six. Or earlier, at the end of any round in which nothing of yours
— no figure and no beast — is still south of the water.

**Anybody may leave.** A figure that walks off the south edge is off the field. It comes
home. It does not roll after the fight unless it was Down and carried off.

### Counting up

A beast is **across** if it is north of the water and no Bailiff's man is in reach of
it. A beast is **taken** if it went off the east edge with one of them. Anything else is
still on the wrong side.

- **Three across** — *You made the crossing.*
- **Two** — *You made it, and it cost you.*
- **One** — *You are still on the road, barely.*
- **None** — *Turn back.*

Nothing here counts the dead, on either side. Nothing in this book ever will.

### Afterwards

Leave the figures exactly where they lie. Then, in this order:

1. For every Down figure on the table — yours and theirs — count its dice, roll, keep
   the highest, read the ladder in §4.
2. Take scars. Write them on the cards, in ink.
3. For every figure that went Down, write one sentence on its card that names something
   that was on the table.
4. Write one sentence in the Road Book: the place, how many got across, who did not
   come home.
5. **Now** tidy the table.

### Sockets left open

These are printed and switched off. That is allowed, and honest.

- **Bringing someone new in.** Between fights you may add a figure to the drove. There
  is no rule for how. Write a line about who they are and where you found them, and give
  them Grit 3 and something to carry.
- **When Ruthin does not come home.** Somebody else has the paper next time. There is
  no rule for who. Give him Grit 3, a sword, and Ruthin's list.
- **Losing the whole thing.** There is no rule for losing the road. A fight can go badly
  and the road goes on. See the note in §9.

## 8 · THE PAGE ON THE TABLE

> ### THE ROLL
> **One die — that is you.**
> **+1 each:** REACH · ABOVE · CAUGHT · STILL
> **−1 each:** COVER · HURT
> **4, 5, 6 = a coin on him.** No floor. No dice, no attack. Never more than five dice.
>
> ### THE ACTIVATION — pick one
> **RUN** two long sticks, nothing else.
> **STAND** move one long stick or stay, then one thing. Didn't move? Claim STILL.
> **WAIT** don't move, don't act — hold a blow. Spend it any time, claiming STILL.
> Coin beside the figure: **heads = a blow in hand. Tails = spent.**
>
> ### THE ONE THING (on a STAND)
> Blow · Shot · **Help** (touch a friend: they claim STILL and are not Alone) ·
> Pick up · Put down · Finish
>
> ### THE ROUND
> Flip for first. Alternate one figure at a time. Beasts drift at the end. Sweep the
> coins. **Six rounds.**
>
> ### GRIT AND DOWN
> Coins = Grit → **DOWN.** Lay it on its side. Face down if it was **ALONE**.
> Down figures cannot be attacked, cannot get up, are not dead.
> **ALONE** = no friend within a short stick. Alone and hurt = you may only go to a
> friend, or WAIT.
>
> ### AFTER THE FIGHT — every Down figure, both sides
> **One die**, +1 if a friend was touching it at the end, +1 if carried off the board,
> +1 if it was **not** Alone when it fell. **Keep the highest.**
> **1** — does not come home · **2** — scar, rolled, misses next fight · **3** — scar,
> rolled · **4–5** — scar, chosen · **6** — no scar, or one of your choosing.
> Then: one sentence on the card. One sentence in the Road Book.
>
> ### THE STICKS
> **Short** = 2 inches. **Long** = 6 inches.
> If it looks reachable, it is. Doubt it once, use the stick. Doubt it twice, flip.
>
> ### THE JUDGES
> The youngest player, and the clock.

## 9 · DESIGN LOG

### Every mechanic, and the principle that forced it

| Mechanic | Forced by | What I rejected, and why |
|---|---|---|
| **Dice pool, 4+, nothing added** | Non-negotiable: *no number is ever added to a die*; DNA 6, *judged by eye* | A d6 + modifiers table (illegal). A varying target number, 4+/5+/6+ (legal, but it is a number you cannot see on the table, so it breaks *a rule you cannot see is not a rule*). Fixed 4+ leaves exactly one number in the whole game. |
| **Four reasons, two penalties, all visible** | DNA 3 · DNA 10 · non-negotiable *a rule you cannot see is not a rule* | A list of eight positional modifiers. That is a table of combinations, which DNA 6 forbids by name. Four is the number a child can hold and it caps the pool at five, which is what stops saturation. |
| **No floor on the pool** | Known Risk 5 | "Never below one die" is the standard fix and it silently deletes Cover and Hurt for exactly the figures who most need to feel them. Zero dice is not a dead end — it is the board telling you to move, before you commit. |
| **RUN / STAND / WAIT, marked with a coin, persisting into the enemy's turn** | DNA 2, the thesis | Action points, and "move-and-shoot with a penalty." Both are modifiers wearing a costume. A commitment that expires at the end of your own activation forecloses nothing; this one is still true when the enemy walks past you. |
| **Droving is free, but a fighting drover is not droving** | DNA 2 · Core loop | A "drive the herd" action competing for the activation. Making it free and automatic is better: the foreclosure is emotional (I stopped to fight and my animals stopped with me) rather than an accounting cost. |
| **Beasts cannot be attacked, at all** | Player Promise: *the animals always come home* | Giving beasts Grit and letting them be shot. The promise is not a tone note; it is a hard rule, and hard rules are cheaper than tone notes. |
| **Down is not dead, and Down is permanent for the fight** | DNA 7 · non-negotiable *falling is not dying* | Getting up mid-fight. It makes the fall cheap, and it doubles the length of the fight, which the clock judges. |
| **Death only from the Finish or from the after-fight roll** | Emotional goal 2 · DNA 8 · *nothing scores a kill* | A "killing blow" result band. A number came up is exactly the death this game must not produce. |
| **The Finish costs a full activation and gains nothing** | Non-negotiable *nothing scores a kill*; DNA 8 | Giving it any benefit at all. A deliberately dominated option — see the lint below. It is dominated on purpose and the rulebook says so out loud. |
| **After-fight: one die per reason, keep the highest** | False Constraint 3 · Emotional goal 5 | A campaign spreadsheet. A recovery table with modifiers. One roll and one sentence is the only persistence that has ever survived, so that is what got built. |
| **"Someone stayed" is worth a whole die** | DNA 9 · Emotional goal 4 | A healer stat, a medic action, a first-aid roll. All of them are a skill check about a person's body. This one is about where somebody was standing when it ended, which costs nothing and is the point. |
| **Alone takes away choices rather than dice** | DNA 9 · Emotional goal 4 | An "Alone: −1 die" modifier. Being weaker is not being alone. Having nowhere to go is. |
| **Face down = fell Alone** | DNA 1, *the table is the memory* | A token, a note, anybody remembering. The figure's own posture carries the state, and it costs nothing and cannot be forgotten. |
| **Scars take and give** | Emotional goal 6 | A wound table of penalties. "A veteran should be visibly changed, not merely worse" is unambiguous. Every scar changes what board dice you can reach for, so scars are still position, not sheet. |
| **✱ mind-scars come off when you write a line naming another figure** | DNA 9 · Emotional goal 5 | A morale stat. A sanity track. A tick box. The sentence is the mechanic. |
| **Enemy: numbered lists in plain English, first true line wins, coin at the bottom** | DNA 4 · False Constraint 7 | A symbol grammar. Plain English does the same job in fewer words and has no collisions, and the handoff explicitly releases the symbol grammar as a false law. |
| **Enoch, who is worth nothing and helps your dying** | DNA 8 · Emotional goal 3 · Emotional goal 7 | A "civilians" rule with a mercy score. *Any procedure for redemption, or for what a person is worth* is an explicit non-goal. Enoch has one mechanical effect and no valuation. The argument is the mechanic. |
| **Rolling after the fight for the Bailiff's men too** | DNA 8 · Emotional goal 3 | Enemy figures as expendable. If they do not carry scars into the next fight they are scenery. |
| **The dial's top notch is the dark one** | DNA 4 · Emotional goal 8 | Hiding the age gate in a content warning. Putting it on a token everybody can see means a child at the table can point at it. |
| **The Law of the Board** | DNA 3 | Trusting people to lay out enough terrain. Principle 3 is arithmetic, and the arithmetic is false on a bare table. |
| **Two sticks, judged by eye, flip when in doubt** | DNA 5 · Acceptable tradeoff: *precision wherever it costs time* | A tape measure and a ruler. |
| **Six rounds** | DNA 6, *the clock is a judge* | Playing until someone breaks. The clock does not exist on paper, so it has to be printed. |

### The lint. Every trap in Known Risks, checked.

**Risk 1 — the rebuild reflex.** No vocabulary was fixed twice. There are twenty-four
nouns in this game: *stick (short, long), coin, die, Grit, coins/Hurt, Down, Alone,
RUN, STAND, WAIT, Reach, Above, Caught, Still, Cover, blow, shot, Help, Carry, Finish,
scar, line, Road Book, Reader, dial, beast, drift, ford.* Nothing has a synonym.
Nothing was renamed. Content was written first and the words were left where they fell.

**Risk 2 — nothing has ever been played.** Every number here is a hypothesis. The three
knobs, in the order you should reach for them: **Grit** (raise everyone to 4 if fights
are too bloody, drop to 2 if they crawl); **the round limit** (six is a guess about an
hour); **the size of the Bailiff's men** (add or remove Twm). Do not touch the 4+ or
the four reasons — those are what make §3 true.

**Risk 3 — architecture crowds out content.** This document is roughly two-thirds
content — a roster of eleven figures with real lines, five enemy brains, six scars, a
complete scenario, and a page of fiction — and one-third rules. There is no governance
layer, no schema, no registry, and no validator. This log is the only meta-text and it
is at the back.

**Risk 4 — position saturates.** Computed in §3 before the law was written, not after.
Max pool 5 → 2.50 average coins against Grit 3 → **no clipping; the last two dice are
worth +18.8% each, dead linear.** Position swing 5.00× against a total sheet swing of
2.50×, so position is worth twice the whole character sheet, and position alone can
reach a pool of zero, which no sheet can. **Found and reported:** Grit 2 figures *do*
saturate mildly at the top (+18.8%, +12.5%), which is why Grit 2 is confined to a dog, a
boy, an old man and one archer — figures who should not be receiving five-dice attacks
in the first place. If playtest shows Grit 2 people being one-shot, the fix is to make
everyone Grit 3 and give the frail ones fewer choices instead.

**Risk 5 — floors quietly delete penalties.** Checked, and the answer is stark: **every
figure in this game rolls a base of exactly one die — 11 of 11, one hundred percent.** A
"never below one" floor would therefore have made Cover and Hurt no-ops for the entire
roster. There is no floor anywhere in this document; both penalties are live for
everybody and can take a pool to zero. The single exception-shaped rule — Gwilym
ignoring the Hurt die — is a named trait on one printed card, not a floor, and it is
visible on the table.

**Risk 6 — non-monotonic result bands.** There are exactly two ladders. (a) *Hits to
coins*: monotone by construction — more dice never yields fewer hits in expectation,
more hits never yields fewer coins, and there are no bands at all. (b) *The after-fight
d6*: each band explicitly permits taking the band below it — 4–5 says "roll instead if
you would rather," 6 says "take a scar of your choosing if you would rather" — so a
higher roll provably contains a lower one and can never be worse. **Caught and killed in
draft:** the first version handled Alone by *keeping the lowest die*, which made "a
friend stayed with you" **raise** the chance of death from 16.7% to 30.6%. It is now
built as a missing bonus rather than a penalty, which is also why it needs no floor.

**Risk 7 — dominated options.** Checked pairwise across every weapon and every stance.

| pair | who wins where |
|---|---|
| Hand vs Long | Long strikes at a short stick and can stop a charge on a WAIT; Hand claims Reach against a touching enemy, which Long cannot. Neither dominates. |
| Hand vs Bow | Bow is unusable within a short stick; Hand is unusable beyond touching. |
| Bow vs Sling | Sling gets Reach and reaches four dice within a long stick, and works in a scrum; Bow reaches three long sticks and works at 12–18 inches where the sling cannot fire at all. |
| Long vs Bow | Different rooms entirely. |
| Bare hands vs everything | Strictly worse at felling — it can never fell — but it is not an option anyone chooses; it is a condition Hen Sam has. His Help is worth a whole die and nobody else's Help moves a long stick. |
| RUN vs STAND vs WAIT | RUN covers twice the ground; STAND is the only one that acts now; WAIT is the only one that acts at a moment of your choosing with the Still die guaranteed. None contains another. |
| Help vs a blow | Help is one die to a friend; a blow is up to five for you. Help wins when the friend is better placed than you are, which is often, and it is the only thing that cancels Alone. |
| **The Finish** | **Strictly dominated on purpose.** It costs a full activation and returns nothing measurable. Flagged here so the lint does not fire silently: this is not an oversight, it is the non-negotiable *nothing scores a kill*, implemented. |

**Risk 8 — the emotional layer keeps being postponed.** Built, in this document, in
rules text: Alone (§2), staying with the fallen (§4), Carrying (§2), scars that give and
take (§4), mind-scars that come off only when you name another person (§4), Enoch (§5),
the dial's visible top notch (§5), the Road Book (§4), and rolling for the enemy's
wounded (§4). Recruitment and the age gate are **not** built; both are printed as open
sockets in §7 and flagged below, because both depend on Open Questions.

**Risk 9 — tone dies in schema.** There is fiction at the top, fiction at the head of
§7, and every trait line on every card says what the person *is*, never what they are
*for*. "He has buried more people than he has fought" is the stat line; the mechanics
are the second half of the sentence.

**Risk 10 — a guard inside the thing it guards.** There is no guard in this document, on
purpose, because one would not survive the next rebuild and the handoff says so. The
only thing protecting this design is that it can be played tonight by people who have
never seen it, and that the Road Book is a piece of paper that lives outside the rules
and cannot be refactored.

### Open Questions — where I stopped, and what I did instead

I have invented no answers. Where a decision needed one, I made the smallest assumption
that lets the game continue and marked it here.

- **Q5, the age gate.** Unanswered. *Smallest assumption:* the dark content is confined
  to one boxed rule (the Finish) and one notch on a visible dial, and **no other rule
  refers to either**, so a table can remove both and play a complete game. That is not a
  content rating, an in-fiction protection, or a deck filter; it is a switch. Whichever
  the real answer turns out to be, it can be built on top of this without touching
  anything else.
- **Q6, a tempo penalty for melee.** Unanswered and untested, so I did not build one.
  The nearest thing in here is that WAIT costs you your movement, and that a blow costs
  your whole activation. If the real answer is "yes, survivable," the hook is obvious:
  a figure that strikes may only STAND, not RUN, on its next activation. It is not in
  the rules.
- **Q7, does a mutual charge break the finding that denying the run-up is the strongest
  defensive act?** Unanswered. The long weapon's WAIT interrupt is the run-up denial and
  it costs a whole activation to arm. If two long-weapon figures both hold and both want
  to spend at the same instant, flip — §2. That is a stopgap so play continues, not a
  ruling.
- **Q8, what replaces the job cheap bodies used to do.** Unanswered, and I want to be
  precise about it: **Help** and **Caught** together give an unarmed figure a real reason
  to go in first, and CAUGHT is worth a full die to the person behind him. But that is
  not the same job as spending the enemy's one answer, because there is no one answer in
  this design to spend. I did not invent one. If the answer is that the enemy should have
  a limited number of Waits per round, the socket is the coin beside the figure.
- **Q9, is there a losing condition?** Unanswered at the campaign level, so there is
  none. The scenario has one — *Turn back* — and the Road Book records it, and nothing
  compounds. Printed as an open socket in §7.
- **Q1** (has it ever been played), **Q2** (which line is the product), **Q3** (the nine
  days), **Q4** (three pages or a software product), **Q10** (who else is at this table)
  did not block anything. On Q4 I took the smallest assumption available: the campaign
  layer in this document is one die and one sentence, and it is half a page long.

### The acceptance test

Four people who have never seen this can sit down with a coffee table, eleven figures,
a fistful of coins and §8, and be rolling inside ten minutes. There is one number to
learn. There are three things a figure can do. The enemy is five numbered lists and a
coin. The fight ends after six rounds whether or not anyone is winning, and then
somebody writes a sentence.

That is the test. Everything above it is in service of it, and anything above it that
is not should be cut before it is defended.
