# THE GREEN ROAD

A skirmish game for two to four people, on a coffee table, in about an hour.

You are moving animals along a road. Other people live on that road. Tonight you have
to get past them, and everyone you brought is somebody's.

**What you need.** Six dice. A handful of coins. A pencil and a sheet of paper. Enough
miniatures for about six people a side, whatever you own — they do not have to match,
they do not have to be painted, and a bottle cap is a cow. Tear a scrap of paper into
small pieces and write FORWARD, STANDING or HANDS FULL on each; you need three or four
of each word.

There is no referee. Nobody sits out. The other side runs itself off a printed page
that anyone can read aloud.

---

## 1 · THE SPAN, AND OTHER THINGS YOU DO NOT MEASURE

Before the first game, one person spreads their hand flat on the table and everyone
looks at it. Thumb-tip to little-finger-tip is a **span**. That hand measures for both
sides all evening. Choose the youngest player's hand; the game shrinks to fit the table
and the child gets to be the ruler, which is only fair, because they are also the judge.

**Touching.** Two figures are touching if their bases touch when you look straight
down. Height does not matter. If they are touching, they can reach each other.

**Facing.** Point the figure's nose. If you can see its face, you are in front of it.
If you are looking at its back, you are behind it.

**Seeing.** Get your eye down level with the figure and look. If you can see the other
figure, you can see it.

**And if you would argue about any of it, flip a coin.** That is not a failure of the
rules. That is the rule. A coin is faster than a tape measure and it is never wrong
twice in the same direction.

Nothing in this game is measured with anything but a hand and an eye. If a decision is
taking more than a few seconds, take the coin out.

---

## 2 · THE ROLL

You never do arithmetic. You pick up dice and you put down coins.

**To strike someone**, take a number of dice equal to your **Hands** (printed on your
card). To shoot someone, take dice equal to your **Eye**.

Then go down this list and **pick up one more die for each thing that is true**:

- **HIGHER** — you are standing on something and they are not.
- **BEHIND** — you are looking at their back.
- **CROWDED** — one more die for each friend of yours, besides you, who is also
  touching them.
- **FOOTING** — they are standing in whatever the scenario named as bad ground.

Then **put one die back for each thing that is true**:

- **COVER** — something solid is between you and them.
- **FAR** — you are shooting, and they are further away than one span.
- **ALONE** — you have no friend within one span of you.

Roll. **Every die showing 4, 5 or 6 is a hit.** Put one coin on their card for each hit.

That is the whole engine. No number is ever added to a die, because nothing is ever
added to anything — the dice are in your hand or they are not.

**If you end up with no dice, you cannot strike.** There is no floor. The ground has
beaten you, and that is allowed to happen. You can always **shove** instead (below).

**Shove.** One die, always one die, never modified by anything. On a 4, 5 or 6 you
either push them one span in any direction, or turn them to face wherever you like.
No harm. This is the line that always works — no figure is ever stuck with nothing to do.

**Shooting into a scrum.** If a friend of yours is touching your target, put one die
back. If you then score no hits at all, put one coin on your friend.

---

## 3 · WOUNDS, FALLING, AND DEATH

**Coins are wounds.** Each hit is one coin on the figure's card.

**When the coins equal the figure's Wounds, it falls.** Lay the miniature on its side.
Take its word-scrap away. It does nothing else. It is not dead. It is on the ground, on
the board, where you left it — and it stays there, in front of everybody, for the rest
of the fight.

A fallen figure can be carried (§5), lifted (Rue only), and found (§8).

**Finish.** ▌*This rule has a black border. See §11.* ▌
A standing figure touching a fallen enemy may, instead of everything else it would do
this activation, finish them. There is no roll. Turn that card face down and put it
aside. That person is dead and will not come back.

Nothing in this game gives you anything for that. There is no line on the scoring sheet
for it. The rules will not tell you when to do it and will not tell you not to. Write
down who did it.

**Death is otherwise only for people nobody came back for** (§8). Both roads to death
run through somebody's decision. No die by itself ever kills anyone.

---

## 4 · ALONE

**A figure with no friend within one span is alone.**

**Shaken.** If a figure takes a wound while it is alone, put a coin on its base — on the
base, not the card, so you can see it from across the room. A shaken figure may not
choose FORWARD. It can stand or it can carry, and that is all.

**Take the coin off the moment that figure begins or ends an activation touching a
friend.** Either of them can do the walking. Bodies mend with coins and time; this one
mends with a person, and only with a person.

---

## 5 · THE TURN

**A round.** Flip a coin to see who activates first. That side activates **one** figure.
Then the other side activates one. Keep alternating. When one side runs out of
un-activated figures, the other side takes all its remaining activations in a row.

At the end of the round, take away all the word-scraps. Shaken coins and wound coins
stay. Flip one coin on the Road Counter (§7).

**Activating a figure.** Do these three things, in this order:

1. **Turn it to face wherever you like.** This is the only time you may.
2. **Put one word-scrap next to it.** This is your commitment. It is on the table until
   the end of the round, where everyone can see it and play against it.
3. **Do what that word allows.**

### FORWARD
Walk one span and strike; or strike and walk one span; or run two spans and do not
strike. Walking is optional — you may just strike.

You are now facing the thing you went at. Everyone else in the world is behind you,
until your next activation, and they will roll an extra die for it. That is not a
penalty printed on FORWARD. That is what going at something means.

### STANDING
You do not move at all.

**The first enemy who strikes you in reach this round takes your Hands dice straight
back, before their harm is placed.** Roll your answer with all the usual position dice.
If your answer fells them, their strike never happens.

Then turn the word-scrap face down. **You have one answer and now it is spent**, for
the rest of the round, no matter how many people come.

You cannot answer arrows. You cannot answer someone striking you from behind — you
were watching the other way.

### HANDS FULL
Run three spans; or walk one span and do one job. Jobs are: pick up a fallen figure,
put one down, open or move a thing the scenario names, lift (Rue), or drive beasts.

You cannot strike. You cannot answer. Anything that comes at you, comes at you.

**Carrying.** Pick up a fallen figure by touching them on HANDS FULL. Stand the two
models side by side and move them together. **While carrying you move one span, not
three.** If the carrier falls, both are on the ground where they stand.

**Stopping.** You must stop moving the moment you come into touch with an enemy figure.

**The far edge.** Any figure or beast that moves off the far edge of the board is out of
the fight and safe. It does not come back. Walking your cattle out is a way to win and
a way to lose an argument, both.

---

## 6 · THE SHOUT

Your side has one, for the whole fight. Put a scrap face up on the table at the start.

**Once, when one of your people falls, you may shout their name.** Every friendly figure
within two spans of them loses its shaken coin and may immediately walk one span toward
the fallen figure. Then turn the scrap face down. That is all you get.

---

## 7 · THE ROAD COUNTER

Five coins in a row at the edge of the table, heads up. Flip one at the end of each
round. When the last one flips, the fight is over. The clock is on the table where the
children can see it, because a clock you cannot see is not a clock.

---

## 8 · AFTER THE FIGHT

Do these in order. It takes five minutes.

**1 · Count the ground.** Score the scenario. Nothing on that sheet counts enemies.

**2 · Ask, for each of your fallen: did anybody come?**

A fallen figure was **FOUND** if any one of these is true:
- someone was carrying them when the last coin flipped, or they were carried off the
  far edge;
- a standing friend of theirs is touching them;
- Wask the gravedigger is touching them (§10).

Otherwise they were **LEFT**.

**3 · Roll one die for each fallen figure.**

| die | FOUND | LEFT ▌*black border* ▌ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a scar | **they did not get up** |
| 2 | a scar | a scar |
| 3 | fine | a scar |
| 4 | fine | a scar |
| 5 | fine | fine |
| 6 | fine | fine |

*fine* means they walk it off. *a scar* means roll again on §9.

Nobody who was found ever dies. That is the point of going back.

**4 · Write the Road.** One line of paper, one sentence, in your own words, for every
figure that fell, scarred, died, or did something you want to remember. Write it on
their card if it belongs to them; on the Road sheet if it belongs to the evening.

That sentence is the campaign. There is no other campaign. There is no software.

**5 · The Toll's dead.** Any enemy card you turned face down is dead. Any enemy who fell
and was not finished goes on your Road sheet, face up, by name. **If the same name is on
your Road twice, you may put that card into your Drove before the next fight.** They keep
their statline. Write one sentence about why they came. There is no other recruitment
rule, no accounting of mercy, and no procedure for deciding what anybody is worth.

**6 · Clear up.** All wound coins and shaken coins come off. Scars stay forever.

---

## 9 · SCARS

Roll one die. Cross out the old number on the card, write the new one, and write your
sentence in the blank underneath.

**1 · The hand that will not close.** Hands −1. Eye +1.
*He learned to do it from further away.*

**2 · The leg.** You never run again — one span, always. Wounds +1.
*He is not going anywhere, so he stopped trying to.*

**3 · The ear.** You can never be given the Shout. You are never shaken.
*Nothing reaches him now. Good or bad.*

**4 · Slow to trust.** You may never be the first figure your side activates in a round.
When you are STANDING, your answer takes one extra die.
*He waits now. He has become very good at waiting.*

**5 · The thing he saw.** Eye −1. Any friend touching you is never alone, however far
away everyone else is.
*People stand near him. Nobody has said why.*

**6 · Harder.** Hands +1. Wounds −1. *If this would take you below one Wound, take scar
2 instead.*
*There is less of him than there was and all of it is edge.*

Hands never goes below 0 — a figure with Hands 0 can still shove, and can still carry,
and is still somebody. Wounds never goes below 1.

**Every scar takes something and gives something**, because that is what surviving does.
A veteran of six fights is not a better figure. They are a different one, and you can
read what happened to them off the front of their card.

**On every card there is a blank line with no label.** No rule in this book reads it.
Write whatever you like there, or nothing. The game does not claim to have modelled the
whole of a person and will not be improved by trying.

---

## 10 · THE TOLL — THE ENEMY THAT RUNS ITSELF

Nobody plays the Toll. Anybody reads it. Pass the page around, one figure each, or let
one person do it all — it comes out the same, which is the whole idea.

**Activate one Toll figure. Read down. Do the first thing that is true. Stop there.**

> **0.** *If I am shaken and I can end a move touching a friend* — HANDS FULL, do that.
> *If I am shaken and I cannot* — STANDING, facing the nearest Drove figure.
>
> **1.** *If a friend of mine is on the ground within two spans and no Drove figure is
> touching me* — HANDS FULL. Go to them, pick them up, and carry them toward my own
> edge.
>
> **2.** *If a Drove figure is touching me* — FORWARD. Strike the one with the most coins
> on it. Tie: the one nearer my own edge. Still tied: flip.
>
> **3.** *If the scenario told me to hold ground, and no Drove figure is past the wall* —
> STANDING, facing the nearest Drove figure.
>
> **4.** *If I can touch a Drove figure by walking one span* — FORWARD. Walk and strike
> the nearest one that is not STANDING. If they are all STANDING, the nearest. Tie: flip.
>
> **5.** *If I have an Eye, and the nearest Drove figure I can see is STANDING* — FORWARD.
> Shoot it. Tie: flip.
>
> **6.** *If I can touch a Drove figure by running two spans* — FORWARD. Run at the
> nearest one that is not STANDING; if they are all STANDING, the nearest. Tie: flip.
>
> **7.** *If I have an Eye and I can see any Drove figure* — FORWARD. Shoot the nearest
> one with no cover. If they all have cover, the nearest anyway. Tie: flip.
>
> **8.** *Otherwise* — FORWARD. Walk one span toward the nearest Drove figure and finish
> facing it.
>
> **9.** *If even that is impossible* — STANDING, facing the nearest Drove figure.

Line 9 always works. Every tie ends in a coin. There is no judgement call anywhere on
this page, and no line requires anyone to decide what the enemy "would" do.

Line 1 is above line 2 on purpose. They go back for their own before they fight you.

### WASK, WHO BURIES WHAT THE ROAD LEAVES

Wask has his own three lines. He never strikes anybody, ever.

> **1.** *If anyone is on the ground within three spans and nobody is touching me* —
> HANDS FULL. Go to the nearest one, either side, and stand touching them.
>
> **2.** *If somebody is touching me* — stand still and do nothing.
>
> **3.** *Otherwise* — HANDS FULL. Walk one span toward the middle of the board.

**Any fallen figure Wask is touching when the last coin flips counts as FOUND — including
yours.** He does not check whose side they are on. He has never done that.

Wask carries nothing, takes nothing, and holds no ground. The rules have nothing further
to say about him, and will not.

### THE DIAL

Agree a number before the fight and leave it written face up on the table. Each level
does everything the level below it does and one thing more, so a higher number is never
accidentally easier.

- **1 — Frightened.** The brain as printed, plus: any Toll figure with a coin on it
  chooses HANDS FULL and runs toward its own edge.
- **2 — Ordinary.** The brain exactly as printed.
- **3 — Awake.** Ordinary, plus: before reading anything, a Toll figure that is alone and
  can end a move touching a friend does that and stops.
- **4 — Hard.** Awake, plus one more figure of the scenario's most common Toll kind.
- **5 — A bad night.** Hard, plus the Toll takes the first *two* activations of every
  round instead of one.

Start at 2. Children beat 1 and remember it. Nobody should meet 5 twice.

---

## 11 · THE BLACK BORDER

Two things in this book have a black border: **Finish** (§3), and the **LEFT** column of
the after-fight roll (§8).

Before your first game, the table agrees out loud whether those two are in play tonight.
That is the entire gate. With them off, nobody at this table can die, and everything else
in the game works exactly as written. With them on, the same game is about what you did
to people. Nobody has to change games to grow up. They change one line and keep their
figures, their scars and their Road.

*(This is the smallest ruling that lets play continue. What the age gate really is — a
rating, a rule inside the fiction, or a filter on the cards — is an open question this
design does not answer.)*

They live here. They were here first. None of them is a monster and none of their cards

### BEASTS

An ox, an ox, and the Nanny. Any three small things. **Hands 0, Eye 0, Wounds 2.**

A beast never activates on its own. **When a figure finishes activating, any beast that
was touching it at the start moves with it, however far it went, and ends touching it.**
That is how a beast travels: somebody walks it.

A beast that takes a wound bolts one span directly away from whatever hurt it. A beast
with two coins lies down and is a fallen figure. A beast is never shaken, is never
finished, and never dies. **A beast that was left behind comes home on its own after
three days and takes a scar.** The animals always come home. That is not a mechanic you
can trade against; it is just true.

## 13 · SCENARIO ONE — THE GATE AT SALLOW FORD

Nine days out. The wall across the ford has been there longer than the road has had a
name, and so has the man standing in the gap of it.

**The board.** Two feet square, or a coffee table.

- Something long and low across the middle — a book, a belt, a row of coasters. That is
  **the wall**.
- A gap in it about two spans wide. That is **the gate**.
- Two things tall enough to stand a figure on, one to each side of the gate. Those are
  **higher**.
- Three or four things a person can get behind, scattered on both halves. Those are
  **cover**.
- The strip of table within half a span of the wall on the Drove's side is **the ford**.
  That is the **bad footing**.

**Forces.** Drove: Maren, Aldis, Old Til, Rue, Sev, Bik, and three beasts. Toll:
Corvain, three Tollers, two Slingers, and Wask. Dial 2.

Nobody counted points. The scenario is the balance and asymmetry is the content.

**Deployment.** The Toll sets up first, and there is no choice in it, so anybody can do
it: Corvain in the gate. One Toller on each high thing. One Toller behind cover in the
middle of the Toll's half. Both Slingers anywhere on the Toll's half behind the wall.
Wask at the very back, at the Toll's own edge.

**Held ground (for brain line 3):** Corvain and the middle Toller hold the gate.

The Drove then places all six figures and three beasts touching its own table edge,
anywhere along it. Each beast must start touching a Drove figure.

The Drove takes the first activation of round one. Flip for it from round two on.

**Length.** Five coins on the Road Counter.

**The ground, at the end.**

- **1 mark to the Drove** for each beast that went off the far edge.
- **1 mark to the Toll** for each beast still on the board, or fallen.
- **1 mark to the Toll** for each Drove figure fallen or dead when the last coin flipped.
- More marks holds the ground. A tie goes to the Toll; they were here first.

**There is no line on this sheet for anyone you put down.** There is no line for Corvain,
and there is no line for Wask.

Then do §8, in order, and write your sentences.

**Two things worth knowing before you start.** You have to stop when you touch an enemy,
and Corvain is standing in the only gap. And the Toll go back for their own — if you put
a Toller down in front of the gate, watch what Corvain does next.

## 14 · THE ARITHMETIC

Principle 3 says the battlefield decides, not the character sheet, and that if the
advantage of position is not worth more than the entire spread of the character sheet,
the rules are lying. Here is the check, done before the law was written.

A die hits on 4+, so it is worth half a hit. **Strikes needed to fell somebody = 2 ×
their Wounds ÷ your dice.**

**The entire spread of the character sheet.** The best card in the game is Corvain
(Hands 3, Wounds 4). The worst is Wask (Hands 1, Wounds 3). On even ground:

- Corvain fells Wask in 2×3÷3 = **2.0 strikes**.
- Wask fells Corvain in 2×4÷1 = **8.0 strikes**.
- **The whole character sheet, best against worst, is worth 4.0×.**

**The same two men, moved.** Give Wask three facts anybody can see — he is up on
something, he is behind Corvain, and one friend is also touching Corvain. Leave Corvain
with one — nobody of his is within a span of him.

- Wask rolls 1+3 = 4 dice. He fells Corvain in 2×4÷4 = **2.0 strikes**.
- Corvain rolls 3−1 = 2 dice. He fells Wask in 2×3÷2 = **3.0 strikes**.
- **The exchange is now 1.5× in Wask's favour.**

From 4.0× against to 1.5× for is a **6.0× swing**, done with four visible facts, against
the widest card gap that exists in the game. **Position out-swings the entire character
sheet by half again, and that is before the answer.** The gravedigger can kill the
gatekeeper. That is what principle 3 has to mean or it means nothing.

Two identical Hands 2 / Wounds 3 people, varying nothing but ground:

| | attacker | defender | exchange |
|---|---|---|---|
| even ground | 2 dice, 3.0 strikes | 2 dice, 3.0 strikes | 1.0× |
| one fact (behind) | 3 dice, 2.0 | 2 dice, 3.0 | 1.5× |
| higher + behind vs alone | 4 dice, 1.5 | 1 die, 6.0 | 4.0× |
| three facts vs alone | 5 dice, 1.2 | 1 die, 6.0 | 5.0× |
| four facts vs alone in cover | 6 dice, 1.0 | 0 dice, never | unbounded |

The sheet's own spread, on even ground, never exceeds 4.0×. One positional fact is worth
1.5×; three are worth 5.0×.

**Saturation (Known Risk 4).** Position saturates when your dice already exceed the
health pool, because after that extra dice buy nothing. Against the standard figure
(Wounds 3) that happens at **6 dice**, where one strike fells them outright:

| dice | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| strikes to fell a Wounds-3 figure | 6.0 | 3.0 | 2.0 | 1.5 | 1.2 | 1.0 | 0.86 |

The standard figure rolls 2. There are exactly **four** advantages on the list. 2 + 4 = 6.
**The advantage list is the length it is because that is where the health pool is.** Every
die of position on the list is still buying real throughput; the seventh would not, so
there is no seventh.

Base lethality is 1.0 hit per strike against a pool of 3 — a third of the pool, not more
than it. The trap in Risk 4 is base lethality exceeding the health pool; this engine sits
at one third of it.

**And past six dice, position keeps paying in a currency that cannot saturate.** A
STANDING figure struck from BEHIND does not answer at all. That is not a die, so no pool
depth can absorb it, and it is why the strongest thing a well-placed figure buys is not
damage but silence.

## 15 · THE TRAPS, CHECKED

Each Known Risk, and what was actually done about it.

**1 · The rebuild reflex.** Nothing here was rebuilt. The vocabulary is one page of plain
words (span, touching, facing, coin, scrap) and it is never revisited. What got the time
was §12, §13 and §14 — a roster, a scenario and the numbers.

**2 · Nothing has been played.** Every number in this document is a hypothesis. The two
that will move first, and the order to move them in: the hit target (4+), and Wounds 3.
Change one, then play, then change the other.

**3 · Architecture crowds out content.** There is no architecture in this document. There
is a roster, a scenario, a brain and a scar list.

**4 · Position saturates.** Checked in §14, arithmetic shown. The advantage list was
sized to the health pool rather than the other way round, and the top of the position
range pays in a non-dice currency.

**5 · Floors quietly delete penalties.** **There is no floor on the dice pool.** Zero dice
means you cannot strike. Roster audit: 6 of 16 cards (38%) have Hands 1, so one
disadvantage removes their strike entirely — Rue, Sev, Bik, both Slingers and Wask. That
is intended, not a bug: an unarmed woman who is alone and reaching over a wall genuinely
cannot do anything to you. Every disadvantage fires at full strength on every card in the
game. The one thing available at any pool is the shove, which is one die and cannot harm
anybody, so it deletes nothing.

There are exactly two floors anywhere in the design, both on the card and neither on a
roll: Hands not below 0 (and a Hands-0 figure can still shove and still carry), Wounds not
below 1. The one place a Wounds floor would have fired — scar 6 on a Wounds-2 figure, which
is 5 of 16 cards — **redirects to a different scar instead of clamping**, so the scar still
costs something.

**6 · Non-monotonic result bands.** There are no result bands. Hits are counted, not
looked up; there is no ladder to be non-monotonic on. The two places a ladder could have
crept in were checked face by face:

- *After-fight roll.* FOUND is at least as good as LEFT on all six faces (1: scar vs dead;
  2: scar vs scar; 3–4: fine vs scar; 5–6: fine vs fine). Going back for someone is never
  worse. Found: 33% scar, 0% dead. Left: 50% scar, 17% dead.
- *The dial.* Every level from 2 upward literally contains the level below it and adds one
  clause, so monotonicity is structural rather than checked. Level 1 removes a clause
  (they break when wounded), which only makes them easier.

**7 · Dominated options.** There is no weapon list at all — what somebody carries is a
sentence on their card and the sentence is not a rule — so the largest surface for this
trap does not exist. What was audited:

- *The three words.* FORWARD moves and strikes but turns your back. STANDING answers first,
  which is the strongest defensive act in the game, but gives up all ground. HANDS FULL
  runs furthest (three spans, versus two) and does the jobs, but cannot fight at all. None
  contains another.
- *Shove vs strike.* Shove does no harm, so it never competes on harm; it does two things
  striking cannot (turn a figure so a friend gets BEHIND, push someone off high ground or
  into the ford).
- *The Drove, pair by pair.* Maren is the best card and pays for it with a printed
  foreclosure — she goes first or not at all. Hanne matches her Hands and holds ground with
  a step. Til trades Eye and running for a Wound and immunity to being shaken. Aldis is the
  only free-shove. Bik is the only figure who does not have to stop. Sixpence is the only
  one who is never alone. Rue is the only one who lifts. Sev is the only Eye 3. No card is
  another card minus something.
- *The Toll.* Marda would otherwise strictly contain the Toller, so she carries a
  restriction: she will not strike anyone on the ground. It matters the moment a spared
  enemy joins your Drove.

**8 · The emotional layer is unbuilt and keeps being scheduled.** It is built, and it is
mechanical rather than promised. Bonds: **alone** and **shaken** (§4) — being on your own
costs a die and can take FORWARD away from you, and only a person removes it. Recruitment:
the Road sheet, §8 step 5, four lines. Mercy: deliberately *not* accounted — Finish gives
you nothing, costs you nothing, and is written down. Age gate: §11, one switch, flagged as
an open question. Loss: §8, one die and one sentence.

**9 · Tone dies in schema.** Every statline in §12 has a sentence that no rule reads. Every
scar has a line of fiction under the numbers. Wask has no mechanical purpose except to make
you argue about him.

**10 · A guard inside the thing it guards.** No continuity mechanism was built, because the
only one that survives is a sheet of paper with sentences on it, and it lives in the box.

## 16 · WHAT IS NOT ANSWERED

Four decisions here lean on questions this design was told not to invent answers to. In
each case the smallest ruling that lets play continue is printed above, and here it is,
flagged.

- **The age gate.** §11: two black-bordered rules and a conversation before the first game.
  Whether the real gate is a rating, an in-fiction protection, or a filter on the cards is
  not settled here.
- **A tempo penalty for melee** — attack, and your next turn is shorter. **Not used.** The
  socket is printed and the subsystem ships switched off: *if you want it, try "a figure
  that struck may not run on its next activation."* Do not turn it on until you have played
  three fights without it. The nearest thing in play is the answer, which costs a STANDING
  figure the rest of its round the first time it fires.
- **Mutual charge.** §5 rules it flatly: whoever activates first strikes first, and there is
  no simultaneous charge. Denying the run-up therefore stays the strongest defensive act,
  because STANDING answers before harm is placed. Whether that finding survives contact with
  a real table is unknown.
- **Losing the whole thing.** There is no campaign loss condition. The road ends when the
  Drove is down to one person, or when the table says the road is walked. That is a
  placeholder and it is doing no work.

One more, answered only far enough to play: **what spends the enemy's answer, now that
answers are cheap?** Here the answer is not cheap — it is one per figure per round, printed
on a scrap, face up. Send the dog in first. He eats it, and the blade behind him lands
unanswered.

## 17 · ONE PAGE — KEEP THIS ONE ON THE TABLE

> **SPAN** — spread the ruling hand, thumb-tip to little-finger-tip. **TOUCHING** — bases
> touch looking straight down. **FACING** — can you see its face, or its back? **SEEING** —
> put your eye on the table. **ARGUING** — flip a coin.
>
> ### THE ROLL
> Take **Hands** dice to strike, **Eye** dice to shoot.
> **+1 die each:** HIGHER · BEHIND · CROWDED (per extra friend on them) · FOOTING
> **−1 die each:** COVER · FAR (shooting, over a span) · ALONE (no friend within a span)
> **4, 5, 6 is a hit. One hit, one coin.**
> No dice = no strike. **Shove** instead: always exactly one die, 4+ pushes one span or
> turns them. Never harms.
> Shooting into a scrum: −1 die; no hits at all puts a coin on your friend.
>
> ### THE ROUND
> Flip for first. Alternate one figure at a time. End of round: scraps off, flip one
> Road coin. Five coins, then stop.
> **Activate:** turn to face → put down one word → do it.
>
> **FORWARD** — walk a span and strike, in either order; or run two and don't. Your back
> is now to everyone else.
> **STANDING** — don't move. The first enemy to strike you in reach takes your Hands
> dice back *before their harm lands*. Then turn the scrap over: spent. No answering
> arrows, no answering from behind.
> **HANDS FULL** — run three spans, or walk one and do a job (pick up, put down, lift,
> open, drive beasts). Carrying: one span. No striking, no answering.
>
> Stop the moment you touch an enemy. Off the far edge is out of the fight and safe.
>
> ### PEOPLE
> Coins = Wounds → **fall**. Lay the figure down. Not dead.
> **Alone** = no friend within a span. Wounded while alone → **shaken coin on the base**;
> no FORWARD until a friend touches you.
> ▌**Finish** — touching a fallen enemy, instead of everything else, no roll, card face
> down. Dead. You get nothing for it. ▌
> **Shout**, once a fight, when one of yours falls: friends within two spans lose shaken
> and step one span toward them.
>
> ### AFTER
> Score the ground. **Found** = carried, or a standing friend touching, or Wask touching.
> Otherwise **left**. One die each:
> **FOUND** — 1–2 scar, 3–6 fine. **LEFT** ▌ 1 dead ▌ 2–4 scar, 5–6 fine.
> Then write one sentence.
>
> ### THE TOLL — first true line, then stop
> 0 shaken → to a friend, else STANDING · 1 friend down within two spans and nobody on me
> → carry him home · 2 someone touching me → strike the one with the most coins · 3 told
> to hold and nobody past the wall → STANDING · 4 can touch by walking → walk and strike
> the nearest who is not STANDING · 5 have an Eye and nearest is STANDING → shoot it ·
> 6 can touch by running two → run and strike the nearest not STANDING · 7 have an Eye and
> can see anyone → shoot the nearest with no cover · 8 walk one span at the nearest and
> face it · 9 STANDING.
> **Every tie: flip.**
> **Wask:** 1 anyone down within three spans and nobody touching me → go stand over the
> nearest, either side · 2 someone touching me → do nothing · 3 walk one span to the
> middle. *He never strikes. Whoever he is touching at the end was found.*
>
> **Dial:** 1 they run when wounded · 2 as printed · 3 the lonely go to a friend first ·
> 4 one more of them · 5 two activations before yours each round.

## 18 · DESIGN LOG

What forced each thing, and what was rejected.

**Dice pools, hits on 4+, no modifiers.** Forced by *no number is ever added to a die*. A
pool is the only common resolution that obeys it literally — you handle dice, you never do
sums. *Rejected:* d6 plus a modifier (illegal); a target number that shifts with position
(that is a number added to a die wearing a hat); step dice, d6→d8→d10 (a family does not
own d10s, and *bespoke components as a requirement* is a non-goal).

**Position is the only modifier, and it is a list of seven visible facts.** Forced by
principle 3 and by *a rule you cannot see on the table is not a rule*. Every entry is
something you can point at with a finger. *Rejected:* skills, traits, keywords, equipment
lists, and anything that would put an advantage on a card instead of on the board. The
weapon list was cut entirely, which also deleted the whole surface of Known Risk 7.

**No floor on the pool; zero dice means no strike.** Forced by Known Risk 5 directly. *"Never
below one die"* was written and then deleted after counting the roster: it would have been a
no-op on 38% of cards. *Rejected:* the floor. Kept instead: the shove, which is a weaker
different action rather than a stronger version of the same one.

**The four advantages, and exactly four.** Forced by Known Risk 4 and the arithmetic in §14.
A fifth would sit past the saturation point against a standard figure. *Rejected:* "target is
HANDS FULL" and "target is down", both cut for that reason; the second is served better by
Finish, which is a decision rather than a bonus.

**Three words on a scrap: FORWARD, STANDING, HANDS FULL.** Forced by *committing forecloses
something* — this is the thesis, so it is structural: one activation, one word, and the word
sits on the table where the enemy plays against it until the round ends. Each closes a real
door — your back, your ground, or your hands. *Rejected:* action points (nothing is
foreclosed, only spent); a first-action-determines-second-action table (that is *depth from a
table of combinations*, which value 6 forbids); a reaction system where anyone may answer
anything (unlimited answers, which is exactly the hole Open Question 8 names).

**Facing is chosen once, on activation.** Forced by the same principle and by *depth comes
from the board*. It also let one rule be deleted: FORWARD does not need a printed "your back
is exposed" clause, because turning toward the thing you charged already does it.

**The answer, and that it is spent.** Forced by *committing forecloses* and by the named
memory of sending a helper in first so the blade behind him lands unanswered. One answer per
figure per round, face up on a scrap, spendable by a cheap body. *Rejected:* an answer every
time you are struck (unlimited, and it doubles the table time); an answer that costs your next
activation (a second commitment stacked on the first, and the clock judged against it).

**Falling, not dying; death by decision.** Forced by *falling is not dying, death is permanent*
and by emotional goal 2. Dice fell people; people kill them. A fallen figure stays on the board
because a body on the table is the argument. *Rejected:* a wound table with a death result on a
1, which is a number coming up rather than somebody deciding.

**Finish scores nothing.** Forced by *nothing scores a kill* and by principle 8. It is written
into the scenario sheet as an absence you can see: there is no line for it. *Rejected:* every
form of mercy accounting, honour track, and redemption arc — the constitution refuses any
procedure for what a person is worth, and a scoring line is exactly such a procedure.

**Found and left.** Forced by *being alone is the worst thing that can happen to someone* and
by emotional goal 1. Death only reaches people nobody came back for, which makes it a decision
too. Checked for monotonicity face by face.

**Alone and shaken.** Forced by principle 9 — *bodies heal with care and time, minds heal with
other people.* A coin on the base, removed only by another figure. It is also a foreclosure:
being shaken takes FORWARD off the table. *Rejected:* a morale statistic, a nerve roll, and
anything that would need a number on the card to model what somebody is feeling.

**Scars: one die and one sentence, each taking and giving.** Forced by emotional goal 6 and by
False Constraint 3, which says outright that the only persistence mechanic that has ever
survived is one roll and one written line. *Rejected:* an experience track, a levelling ladder,
and injury tables that only subtract. A veteran is a different figure, not a worse one.

**The unlabelled blank line on every card.** Forced by *the system never claims to have modelled
the whole of a person.* It is a blank line and nothing else — no formal primitive, no custodian,
no validator, per False Constraint 6.

**The Toll's brain in plain English, first true line wins, coin at the bottom.** Forced by
*nobody at the table is the referee*, and by False Constraint 7, which releases the symbol
grammar. Line 1 is rescue, above fighting, because principle 8 says the enemy is a person and
the cheapest way to say so is to make them go back for each other while you watch. *Rejected:* a
priority-scoring AI, a deck of behaviour cards, and any symbol set — plain sentences did the
same job in fewer words with no collisions.

**Wask.** Forced by emotional goal 7 — the room has to argue out loud about something the rules
refuse to settle. He never fights, he tends both sides' fallen, killing him gains you nothing
and costs you the safety of your own people, and the book explicitly declines to advise you.
*Rejected:* giving him a rule that resolves the argument.

**The span, and the coin.** Forced by *judged by eye, settled in seconds*, by *precision wherever
it costs time*, and by principle 4's coin at the bottom of everything. *Rejected:* inches,
centimetres, and a measuring stick — a stick is a bespoke component and a hand is not.

**Beasts that never die.** Forced by the player promise: the animals always come home. It is not
a balance decision and does not trade against anything.

**One setting, finished.** Forced by False Constraint 5. There is one road, one gate, one man
standing in it, and thirteen named people. There is no second setting and no setting-neutral
layer.
