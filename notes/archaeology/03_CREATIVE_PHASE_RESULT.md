# CREATIVE PHASE — RESULT AND CONVERGENCE ANALYSIS

**Status: UNRATIFIED FINDING.** Companion to `01_CREATIVE_PHASE_HANDOFF.md`.

Three design teams were given the handoff and nothing else. Sealed rooms: each worked
in an empty directory containing one file, forbidden from reading the corpus, the
repositories, the vault, or anything named after them. They had the handoff's 2,010
words and general knowledge of tabletop games.

Three, rather than one, to answer a question that one cannot: **which parts of this
design are forced by the DNA, and which are one designer's taste?** Anything three
blind derivations land on independently was caused by the constitution. Anything only
one produced was a choice.

Output: three complete playable designs, 24,267 words total.

## 1 · UNANIMOUS — forced by the DNA

Every one of these appears in all three designs, independently derived.

| # | Convergence | Which principle forced it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **All three named the game *The Green Road*.** | Forced by the handoff — "stewardship on a green road" is the stated Core Fantasy. Not evidence of anything; recorded for completeness. |
| 2 | **All three set it on a road, moving animals, with people who want to take them.** | Core Fantasy + "you take people out and bring some back changed" |
| 3 | **Dice pool. One fixed target number for the entire game — 4+ on d6. Modifiers are dice you pick up or put back, never a number.** | Non-negotiable: *no number is ever added to a die at the table* |
| 4 | **Positional advantages are a short list of yes/no facts you answer by looking at the table** (Above/Behind/Caught/Still vs Cover/Hurt/Far/Alone). | DNA 1 *the table is the memory* + DNA 5 *judged by eye* |
| 5 | **Hits map 1:1 to wounds, and wounds are coins on the base.** No damage dice, no arithmetic. | Non-negotiable + DNA 1 |
| 6 | **No floor. Zero dice means the attack does not happen.** All three refused "never below one die" explicitly, and all three named it as a Known Risk they checked. | DNA 3 — *deny the geometry, not a budget* |
| 7 | **One answer per figure per round, and striking spends it.** A: a commitment coin, heads = a blow still in hand. B: a word-scrap that turns over when spent. C: a Guard coin, spend to strike *or* to answer, never both. | DNA 2 — *committing forecloses something* |
| 8 | **The commitment is a visible object that persists into the enemy's turn** — the enemy plays against it. | DNA 2 + *a rule you cannot see on the table is not a rule* |
| 9 | **Down is not dead, and Down never gets up mid-fight.** The figure lies on the board where it fell. | Emotional goal 1 |
| 10 | **Death has exactly two sources, both outside the roll:** a deliberate Finish that costs an activation and is scored by nothing, and an after-fight roll. | DNA 8 + *nothing scores a kill* |
| 11 | **The after-fight roll is modified by whether anyone was with them.** A: +1 if a friend was touching, keep highest. B: those nobody came back for. C: two dice keep higher if someone stood next to them. | DNA 9 — *being alone is the worst thing that can happen to someone* |
| 12 | **A tender who belongs to the enemy, tends both sides' fallen, is worth nothing to any victory condition, and whose presence counts *your* fallen as found.** A: Enoch. B: Wask the gravedigger. C: Adder Pye. All three: killing him gains nothing and costs you, and all three refuse to advise. | Emotional goal 3 *the enemy should be someone* + emotional goal 7 *the room should have to argue* |
| 13 | **Animals never die.** Never rolled for, or cannot be killed. | Player Promise — *the animals always come home* |
| 14 | **The enemy brain is a numbered plain-English list. First true line wins. The last line is always true. Every tie ends in a coin.** No judgement calls anywhere. | Non-negotiable: *nobody at the table is the referee* |
| 15 | **A visible difficulty dial that never touches a die** — three to five notches, each containing the one below. | DNA 4 + the arithmetic must hold at every setting |
| 16 | **All three computed the position payoff and all three passed principle 3** — position worth roughly **2× the entire character sheet** (A: exactly 2.0×; B: 1.5× on top of a 4.0× sheet swing; C: 1.8–2.3×). | DNA 3, and the Known Risk about saturation |

**Sixteen convergences. Numbers 6, 7, 11 and 12 are the significant ones** — see §4.

## 2 · DIVERGENT — taste, not law

| Question | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Measurement | inches, 24″ board | **a *span* — the youngest player's spread hand**, so the game scales to the table | inches |
| Weapons | kept, audited pairwise | **cut the weapon list entirely** to remove the domination surface | kept, made to trade (SHORT punishes a spent answer, LONG denies the run-up) |
| Who runs the enemy | a third player, the **Reader** — explicitly not an opponent, "the weather" | both players, no GM | both players, no GM |
| Health | Grit 2–3 | Wounds 3–4 | Grit 3 |
| Saturation control | hard-cap the pool at 5 by having only four advantages | **size the advantage list to the health pool** (2 base + 4 = the 6-die ceiling) | let the marginal die decay and prove it never nears zero |
| Isolation, shown | **lay the figure face down if it fell Alone** | tracked on both layers | tracked on both layers |

B's *span* is the most distinctive single invention across the three, and it is a direct
literal reading of a non-negotiable: if the youngest player is a judge, make the
youngest player the ruler.

## 3 · THE ONE GENUINE DISAGREEMENT

**A and C built the same mechanic in opposite directions, and both are defensible.**

The after-fight roll for a figure who fell with nobody near them:

- **C shipped it as a penalty.** Nobody with you → roll two, keep the lower → **30.6%
  dead**. Someone with you → keep the higher → **2.8% dead**. An eleven-fold swing, and
  C names it the biggest emotional lever in the game.
- **A built that, caught it in lint, and rebuilt it.** A's note: framing it as a penalty
  meant *a friend staying with you* moved death from 16.7% to 30.6% — the mechanic read
  as punishing the isolated rather than rewarding the companion. A recast it as a
  **missing bonus** instead: base one die, +1 for each of three reasons, keep highest.
  Same odds at the extremes; opposite meaning.

Both serve DNA 9. They disagree about whether being alone should be *punished* or
whether being accompanied should be *rewarded*. The evidence in the corpus does not
settle it — and it is exactly the kind of question a table answers in one session.

## 4 · WHAT THIS CORROBORATES ABOUT THE ORIGINAL CORPUS

Four of the convergences are not generic tabletop design. They are specific, and each
matches something the original corpus produced, diagnosed, or lost.

**4.1 — The spent answer (convergence 7).** All three built a single visible token per
figure per round, consumed by striking, which makes *sending someone in first so the
blade behind them lands unanswered* a real tactic. The First Corpus struck exactly this
as architecturally redundant — *"Position was already answering that question"* — and
knowingly logged the loss of chaff sequencing. The Campaign line independently rebuilt
it as `Overwhelm` and authored it into the enemy trees. **Three blind derivations now
make it four independent arrivals.** The ruling that struck it was architecturally
correct; the job the mechanic was doing was real and was never reassigned.

**4.2 — The refusal of the floor (convergence 6).** All three explicitly rejected
"never below one die" and all three flagged it as a checked risk. In the Table
Companion, that floor silently makes cover, Grease, Blinded, Prone and Brace do nothing
against every ATK-1 model — which is the entire non-boss roster. The handoff carried
the risk as an abstraction; three designers independently treated it as disqualifying.

**4.3 — The tender (convergence 12).** All three invented an enemy non-combatant who
tends both sides' fallen, is worth nothing to victory, and whose presence counts your
own dead as found. None had seen the Pale Sexton. This was reconstructed from one
emotional goal and one line about the room having to argue out loud.

**4.4 — Position at 2× the sheet (convergence 16).** All three computed it and all
three landed near the same magnitude, which is also the magnitude the Core Rulebook
achieves (≈×3.1 at the rear arc) and the one the Vault lost (+5–47%). The number is not
arbitrary; it appears to be what the principle actually requires.

## 5 · THE HONEST CAVEAT

Three instances of the same model, from near-identical prompts, share priors. Some of
this convergence is shared training rather than the DNA doing work. That is a real
limit on the method and it should not be waved away.

Two things push back on it:

1. **The convergences are specific, not generic.** "Use a dice pool" is a shared prior.
   "Refuse a dice floor because a floor deletes penalties for the models that roll one
   die" is not. Nor is a worthless enemy gravedigger whose presence redeems your own
   casualties.
2. **Three of the four significant convergences match things the original corpus
   independently produced or independently diagnosed.** That is corroboration from
   outside the model's priors — the designer arrived at the spent answer, the tender,
   and the 2× position payoff on his own, years of work apart, in engines that share no
   vocabulary.

The claim this supports is narrow and worth stating precisely: **the handoff carries
enough of the design to regenerate its most characteristic parts.** That is what a
constitution is for.

## 6 · WHERE THE DESIGNS ARE

`scratchpad/creative/designer-{a,b,c}/DESIGN.md` — three complete playable cores, each
with a resolution engine, turn structure, position audit with arithmetic shown, a
printed enemy brain, a starter roster with real statlines, a first scenario, a one-page
reference, and a design log tracing each mechanic to the principle that forced it.

All three checked every Known Risk in the handoff and stated that they did. Two found
real defects in their own drafts and rebuilt (A on the isolation framing; C on a
spend-your-hits menu that would have introduced non-monotonic bands).

None answered an Open Question. All three flagged the smallest assumption that let play
continue and printed the socket.
